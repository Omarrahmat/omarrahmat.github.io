Black Country Fostering launches as four councils pool foster carer recruitment

Walsall, Dudley, Sandwell and Wolverhampton have launched Black Country Fostering, a shared front door for people interested in fostering, backed by Department for Education funding.

Four councils in the West Midlands have launched Black Country Fostering, a regional partnership for recruiting and supporting foster carers. Walsall Council announced the launch on 5 November 2026. The partnership brings together Walsall Council, Dudley Council, the City of Wolverhampton Council and Sandwell Children's Trust, which runs children's services for Sandwell.

The hub is one of the regional fostering hubs being set up under the Department for Education's programme to strengthen council fostering services. Walsall's earlier announcement in July said the Black Country was one of four new hubs in England named by Josh MacAlister, the Minister for Children and Families. Dudley Council said in September that the partnership had been awarded more than £1.5 million from the Department for Education for the next two years, and that Walsall Council would lead it. The councils say the aim is to increase the number of local foster carers, keep more of the carers they have, and reduce reliance on more costly independent fostering agencies.

For people thinking about fostering, the main change is a single point of contact across the four areas. According to the launch announcement, enquirers can get information, guidance and advice in one place, and prospective carers are offered local training, financial support and dedicated guidance from fostering professionals. Dudley Council described dedicated officers who will guide applicants from their first enquiry through to approval, with extra support for families in their first year of fostering. The hub is also starting a fostering ambassador scheme so that people considering fostering can talk to experienced foster carers, and it will run marketing campaigns aimed at common misconceptions.

Those misconceptions were set out in Sandwell Council's announcement in October, which drew on government research for the national fostering recruitment campaign. Around one in five people wrongly believe foster carers must earn above a certain income (22%), own their home (20%) or be married or in a long-term relationship (18%). Previous childcare experience or qualifications was the most commonly chosen perceived requirement in England, picked by 29% of respondents in the West Midlands. None of these is a requirement to foster.

Existing foster carers with the four services are promised training, development opportunities and continued support through the partnership. Councillor Lisa Jones, Walsall's portfolio holder for children, education and skills, called the launch an exciting opportunity. Councillor Wayne Little, Dudley's cabinet member for children and young people, said working with neighbouring councils would strengthen recruitment and support and make it easier for people to take the first step.

The launch announcement links the partnership to the government's national ambition to create 10,000 additional places for children who need safe, stable and loving homes. People living in the Black Country can find out more on the Black Country Fostering website or by calling its recruitment team on 0800 923 3706.
