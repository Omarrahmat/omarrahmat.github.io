Warwickshire launches 'Written Together' campaign to recruit specialist foster carers

Warwickshire County Council has launched a campaign and short film, Written Together, asking teachers, health workers, social workers and others in caring professions to consider its specialist fostering schemes.

Warwickshire County Council's fostering service announced on 11 November 2026 that it has developed an enhanced offer for specialist foster carers, the people who look after children and young people with more complex needs. The council says the offer brings dedicated support, specialist training and greater financial recognition for carers in these roles, and it has launched a recruitment campaign called Written Together to find them.

At the centre of the campaign is a short film shot at locations around the county. It follows a teacher, a social worker and a healthcare professional, and is aimed at people whose work already involves caring for or supporting others: education, health, social care, youth services and similar fields. The council names five schemes that need carers: Breathing Space, Solo, Emergency Duty, Treehouse and Parent and Child fostering. Councillor Wayne Briggs, the council's portfolio holder for children and families, education and SEND, said specialist foster carers can help every child have the chance to thrive.

The council's fostering website explains what several of these roles involve. Breathing Space carers take children aged 0 to 18 who need a safe home straight away, usually for up to 28 days while their needs are assessed and a longer-term plan is made; the council asks for a free bedroom and at least one carer available during the day. Solo carers look after one child only, a young person who has experienced trauma and might otherwise be placed in residential care, and the council looks for significant fostering experience and confidence working therapeutically. A Treehouse carer acts as a hub for six to nine other foster families, offering sleepovers, short breaks and mentoring so that those placements stay steady.

Warwickshire describes Solo, Breathing Space, Treehouse and Parent and Child fostering as salaried options. Its scheme pages list support including a dedicated supervising social worker, regular supervision and support groups, therapeutic and multi-agency advice, a family support worker and out-of-hours help. The pages do not state the fees themselves; they point to the council's 2026/27 finance guide for the rates.

For people thinking about fostering, the campaign is a reminder that specialist and salaried roles exist alongside mainstream fostering, and that experience from a caring job can be directly relevant. These roles usually ask more of carers, in availability, experience or training, and come with more structured support and higher payments in return. If one appeals, ask the service for its current finance guide, what the role pays when no child is placed, and how rest periods and leave work in practice.

The council says people can watch the Written Together film and read about each scheme on its fostering website, and its fostering team takes enquiries by phone and email.
