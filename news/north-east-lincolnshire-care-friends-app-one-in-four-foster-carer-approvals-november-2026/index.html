North East Lincolnshire says referral app now brings one in four of its new foster carers

Fostering NEL, North East Lincolnshire Council's fostering service, says five new foster carers have come through the Care Friends referral app since it was introduced, a quarter of its recent intake.

North East Lincolnshire Council announced on 24 November 2026 that a phone app built around personal recommendations is now behind one in four of the new foster carers approved by its fostering service, Fostering NEL. The app, Care Friends, lets existing foster carers share information about fostering with friends, family and their wider circle, and the council says it was the first local authority in England to adopt it.

According to the council, the app was introduced in May of last year. Since then, five new foster carers have been recruited through it, which the council describes as 25 per cent of its recent intake. All five have completed the assessment process and are now fostering.

The scheme works on word of mouth. A foster carer who recommends fostering talks to the person first, then shares a listing through the app so that the interested person can speak to someone in the fostering team. Carers earn points for referrals, each worth £1, and can receive up to £500 for each person they refer. The council quotes two of its carers saying the app is simple to use and that they plan to spend their points on treats, including a trip with the children they look after.

Ann-Marie Matson, the council's director of children's services, said the council wants to find new ways of reaching people who have never thought about fostering, and described its foster carers as its best ambassadors.

What makes the announcement useful is that the council has put a figure on it: a quarter of its recent approvals came through recommendations made in the app. Its account also says that each referral starts with a conversation between the existing carer and the person interested, before the fostering team is involved.

For anyone thinking about fostering, the practical point is that talking to a current foster carer is one of the most useful first steps: they can tell you what the assessment is like, what support they actually get and what a typical week involves. If you live in North East Lincolnshire, the council asks people to start at its fostering page, nelincs.gov.uk/fostering. Elsewhere, ask any service you contact whether you can speak to one of its carers before you apply, and current carers may want to ask their own service whether it runs a referral scheme and what it pays.
