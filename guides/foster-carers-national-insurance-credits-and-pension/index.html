Do foster carers get National Insurance credits? Fostering and your State Pension

Approved foster carers can claim Class 3 National Insurance credits for every week they are approved, so years spent fostering can still count towards the State Pension. Here is who qualifies, how Class 2 and Class 4 work for carers, how to apply on form CF411A, and what differs across the UK.

The short answer

Yes. If you are an approved foster carer you can apply for Class 3 National Insurance credits for each week you are approved, and those credits count towards your State Pension. GOV.UK lists foster carers, and kinship carers in Scotland, among the people who can apply for Class 3 credits for any period on or after 6 April 2010. HMRC's own National Insurance manual explains that a credit is given for each week in which a person is a foster parent for any part of that week.

This matters because many foster carers pay little or no National Insurance on their fostering income. Foster carers are treated as self-employed, but qualifying care relief often reduces the taxable profit from fostering to a small figure or to nothing. Without either a high enough profit or a credit, a year spent fostering could leave a gap in your National Insurance record, and gaps can mean a smaller State Pension later.

The credits are not automatic. You have to apply, usually once a year, with a letter from your fostering service confirming the weeks you were approved. The rest of this guide explains how the system fits together, when you need the credits and when you do not, and how to check your record. It is general information, not personal tax or pensions advice: HMRC, the Pension Service and your fostering service can confirm your own position.

Why your National Insurance record matters for your pension

Your State Pension is built from qualifying years on your National Insurance record. GOV.UK says a qualifying year is one in which you paid National Insurance, received National Insurance credits or paid voluntary contributions. Under the new State Pension you usually need at least 10 qualifying years to get anything at all, and 35 qualifying years to get the full amount if your record started after April 2016. GOV.UK currently gives the full new State Pension as £241.30 a week, and it rises each year in line with the highest of earnings growth, prices or 2.5%.

Each missing year therefore has a cost. A carer who fosters for ten or fifteen years during their working life, while paying no National Insurance on the fostering income, could otherwise end up several years short of a full pension. National Insurance credits for foster carers exist to stop that from happening: they fill the year in the same way as paid contributions would for State Pension purposes.

One limit to keep in mind: Class 3 credits count towards the State Pension (and, according to LITRG, some bereavement benefits) but not towards contribution-based benefits such as Jobseeker's Allowance, Employment and Support Allowance or Maternity Allowance. The Fostering Network makes the same point: credits for foster carers count towards your State Pension only.

How National Insurance works for self-employed foster carers

HMRC's manual says foster carers are generally self-employed for National Insurance and can be liable for Class 2 and Class 4 contributions, depending on their profits. Most carers register with HMRC as self-employed after approval and complete a Self Assessment return each year. The Fostering Network says you can be charged a penalty if you do not register within six months of the end of the tax year in which you were approved, and Foster Wales notes that only one carer in a household needs to register for the fostering income.

The profit that counts is worked out after qualifying care relief, the special tax relief for foster carers. Under the simplified method, your taxable profit is your fostering receipts minus the qualifying amount; if your receipts are below the qualifying amount, there is no taxable profit at all. Our separate guide to qualifying care relief explains how the qualifying amount is calculated. Because the relief is generous, many carers end up with a very small taxable profit, or none.

The rules on Class 2 changed in April 2024. HMRC's manual says that from 6 April 2024 nobody is liable to pay Class 2. Instead, if your self-employed profit is at or above the Small Profits Threshold, you are treated as having paid Class 2, so the year counts on your record without any payment. If your profit is below the threshold, nothing is due, but you can choose to pay Class 2 voluntarily. Class 4 is a separate charge that only applies to higher profits.

  • Small Profits Threshold for 2026 to 2027: £7,105 a year. At or above this, Class 2 is treated as paid (GOV.UK).
  • Voluntary Class 2 rate for 2026 to 2027: £3.65 a week, if your profit is below £7,105.
  • Class 4 for 2026 to 2027: 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% on profits above £50,270.
  • Class 3 voluntary rate for 2026 to 2027: £18.40 a week, for people who are not self-employed or who want to fill other gaps.

When you need the credits, and when you do not

Put simply, the credits help most when your taxable profit from fostering is below the Small Profits Threshold and you have no other earnings that already give you a qualifying year. That is a common position for carers whose fostering income is covered by qualifying care relief. The Fostering Network sets out three choices for carers below the threshold: do nothing (which can leave a gap and may affect contributory benefits), pay Class 2 voluntarily, or apply for National Insurance credits for each week you were approved.

You may not need to apply if the year is already covered another way. For example, if you also work as an employee and earn enough to pay or be treated as paying Class 1 contributions, or if your fostering profit is above the Small Profits Threshold so that Class 2 is treated as paid, the year should already count. GOV.UK also says some people receive Class 3 credits automatically: parents registered for Child Benefit for a child under 12, and people who get Universal Credit. If you have your own child under 12 and claim Child Benefit, you may already be getting credits for those years.

There is one important exception. GOV.UK says married women and widows paying the reduced rate of National Insurance usually cannot get credits. If you are unsure which applies to you, your National Insurance record will show whether a year is already a qualifying year before you apply.

  • Profit below £7,105 and no other qualifying earnings: apply for foster carer credits, or consider voluntary Class 2.
  • Profit at or above £7,105: Class 2 is treated as paid, so the year should already count.
  • Employed as well and earning enough: the year may already count through your job.
  • Child Benefit for your own child under 12, or Universal Credit: you may already get Class 3 credits automatically.

Who counts as a foster carer for the credits

HMRC's test is that you are approved as a foster carer, not that a child is living with you. The manual gives a credit for each week in which you are a foster parent for any part of that week. So weeks between placements, while you remain approved, can still count. Action for Children, one of the large voluntary fostering providers, describes it the same way: you apply for one credit for each approved week, once a year, looking back.

The regulations behind the credits list the approvals that qualify. HMRC's manual names foster parents approved under fostering services regulations, foster carers and kinship carers approved under the Looked After Children (Scotland) Regulations 2009, and foster parents approved under the Northern Ireland foster placement regulations. That is why GOV.UK says the credits are open to foster carers, and to kinship carers in Scotland.

Because the rule is about approval, the credits are personal to each approved carer. If you foster as a couple, ask your fostering service whether its confirmation letter covers you both, and check with HMRC how to apply for each of you. Private fostering arrangements, where a parent makes their own arrangement with another family, are not approvals by a fostering service, so they are unlikely to qualify.

How to apply: form CF411A

You apply on HMRC's form CF411A, 'Apply for National Insurance credits if you're a parent or carer'. GOV.UK says foster and kinship carers can apply online by signing in to HMRC's online service, which gives you a reference number to track the claim, or by post. The postal form cannot be saved part-way through, so have your details ready, and post any supporting evidence with it.

You need evidence from your fostering service. The Fostering Network and Action for Children both say you will need a letter from your service confirming the period you were approved in the tax year you are claiming for. If you upload the letter online it must be a PDF or JPEG of 5MB or less. Postal forms go to PT Operations North East England, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1AN.

Timing matters. HMRC's manual says the application must reach HMRC before the end of the tax year after the one you are claiming for, or within any further time HMRC considers reasonable. For example, credits for the 2025 to 2026 tax year should be claimed by 5 April 2027. The simplest routine is to ask your fostering service for the letter soon after each tax year ends on 5 April, then send the form straight away.

  • Ask your fostering service for a letter confirming your approval dates for the tax year.
  • Complete form CF411A online through HMRC's service, or download it and post it.
  • Attach or enclose the letter (online uploads: PDF or JPEG, 5MB or less).
  • Apply before the end of the following tax year.
  • Check your National Insurance record a few months later to see the credits.

Fostering before April 2010: Home Responsibilities Protection

Before the current credits began, foster carers could be protected by Home Responsibilities Protection (HRP). HMRC's manual says approved foster carers are entitled to HRP for any tax year between 6 April 2003 and 5 April 2010 throughout which they were approved. As with credits, a child did not have to live with the carer all year; being approved for the whole tax year was enough.

HRP had to be claimed. GOV.UK's credits page now points people who fostered between 6 April 2003 and 5 April 2010 to its Home Responsibilities Protection page to claim, and HMRC's manual says claims were made on form CF411 with a letter from the council or agency confirming approval for the whole tax year. If you fostered in those years and your record shows gaps, it is worth checking whether HRP was ever applied.

Checking your record and planning ahead

The simplest check is your online National Insurance record and State Pension forecast on GOV.UK. The record shows your contributions and credits up to the start of the current tax year, which years are not full, whether voluntary contributions would help your forecast, and what it would cost. If a fostering year is shown as a gap, first check whether you applied for credits for that year and whether the deadline has passed, before paying anything voluntarily.

Voluntary contributions are an option if credits are not available. GOV.UK says Class 2 for the previous tax year and Class 3 for the previous two tax years are charged at the rates for those years, and older years at the current rate. Whether paying makes sense depends on how many qualifying years you already have and your State Pension age, so check your online forecast, which shows whether a payment would help, before you pay.

National Insurance stops being due as you reach State Pension age: The Fostering Network says Class 2 stops at State Pension age and Class 4 stops from the start of the following tax year. Fostering income and any workplace or private pension you draw are separate matters for your tax return, and Foster Wales reminds carers to declare pension income there. For decisions about your own pension savings, speak to a regulated financial adviser.

Across the UK

England
Foster carers approved by a council or independent fostering agency can apply for Class 3 credits on form CF411A for each approved week since 6 April 2010. GOV.UK's guide to support for foster carers in England confirms carers may be entitled to National Insurance credits that count towards the State Pension.
Scotland
The credits come from UK-wide regulations, which name Scottish approvals, so the same Class 3 credits and CF411A form apply. Scotland's difference is that approved kinship carers, as well as foster carers, are named in the regulations and on GOV.UK as able to apply for Class 3 credits.
Wales
The same UK-wide rules apply. Foster Wales explains that carers are self-employed, that Class 2 is treated as paid when taxable fostering income is above the Small Profits Threshold, and that carers below it can pay voluntarily or apply for credits.
Northern Ireland
Foster carers approved under the Northern Ireland foster placement regulations qualify for the same Class 3 credits. nidirect lists registered foster carers among those who can get credits towards the State Pension; pension questions go to the Northern Ireland Pension Centre.

Common questions

Do foster carers pay National Insurance?

Foster carers are treated as self-employed. Since April 2024 nobody has to pay Class 2: if your fostering profit after qualifying care relief is at or above the Small Profits Threshold (£7,105 for 2026 to 2027), Class 2 is treated as paid. Class 4 is only charged on profits above £12,570. Many carers' profits are below these levels, so they pay nothing.

Does fostering count towards my State Pension?

It can. If your fostering profit is too low to count, you can apply for Class 3 National Insurance credits for each week you were approved as a foster carer since 6 April 2010. Each full year of credits counts as a qualifying year for the State Pension.

Do I get credits when I have no child placed with me?

HMRC's test is approval, not placement. A credit is given for each week in which you are an approved foster carer for any part of the week, so weeks between placements can count while you stay approved.

How do I claim National Insurance credits as a foster carer?

Ask your fostering service for a letter confirming your approval dates for the tax year, then apply on form CF411A, online through HMRC or by post. Apply before the end of the following tax year.

I fostered before 2010. Can those years count?

For 6 April 2003 to 5 April 2010, approved foster carers could get Home Responsibilities Protection for tax years in which they were approved throughout. GOV.UK points carers from those years to its Home Responsibilities Protection page to claim.

Should I pay voluntary National Insurance instead?

It depends on your record, which only HMRC and the Pension Service can confirm. Check your online National Insurance record and State Pension forecast first: if credits are available for a fostering year, they cost nothing, and the forecast shows whether a voluntary payment would actually raise your pension.
