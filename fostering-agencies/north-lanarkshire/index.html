Fostering in North Lanarkshire

Children in care in North Lanarkshire are looked after by North Lanarkshire Council's own fostering service and by the independent agencies shown above. The council's fostering, adoption, supported care and kinship teams are based at the Children's Carers' Centre on Mitchell Street in Airdrie, which has training and meeting rooms, a crèche and a drop-in service for carers. The council says foster carers can use the centre for support and for its training programme.

North Lanarkshire has used Foster Care Fortnight to ask more local people to come forward, and its information events have been held at the Airdrie centre, where the team answers questions about how to become a carer. Enquiries go to the council's fostering and adoption team, by freephone or by email, and the council also points people to the Fostering and Adoption website for more detail.

As in the rest of Scotland, every council must pay foster carers at least the Scottish Recommended Allowance, which covers the cost of looking after the child. The Scottish Government raised it for 2026-27, backdated to 1 April 2026, to £177.68 a week for children aged 0 to 4, £206.71 for ages 5 to 15, and £283.35 for young people aged 16 and over. Any fee paid on top for the carer's time and skills is set by each fostering service, so it differs between the council and agencies. Applying to foster does not normally cost anything.

North Lanarkshire pays kinship carers the same weekly amounts, which reflects the national parity between fostering and kinship allowances. Before you decide, speak to the council and to two or three of the services above, and ask each what fee, training and out-of-hours support they offer. Neighbouring South Lanarkshire has its own page on this site.

Sources: Children's Carers' Centre (North Lanarkshire Council); Supporting Foster Care Fortnight (North Lanarkshire Council); Ever wanted to be a foster carer?
