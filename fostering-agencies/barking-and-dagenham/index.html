Fostering in Barking and Dagenham

Barking and Dagenham Council runs its own fostering service from Barking Town Hall and is looking both for new applicants and for experienced carers who want to transfer to it. It is shown above with the independent agencies that list the borough as an area they cover. The council is also one of the north-east London boroughs in Local Community Fostering, a shared front door that handles first enquiries and screening before passing applications to the council.

The council says carers could receive up to £700 per child each week and look after up to three children, with the exact amount worked out for each carer according to the children's ages. New carers complete a training portfolio in their first year, have a fostering buddy for that year and monthly supervision from their own supervising social worker, and can join a new carers' group and a diversity support group. Out-of-hours support is staffed by the fostering team itself.

The service runs the Mockingbird family model, in which a hub home with two spare bedrooms supports up to ten nearby fostering households with respite and regular get-togethers, and it has received a National Fostering Award recognising that work.

You do not need to be married, and neither sexuality nor disability is a bar. A house or flat is fine if it is in your name and has the room children need. The council generally prefers applicants to be at least 25, and for most types of fostering one carer in the home needs to be free from work. A past conviction does not rule you out, but should be raised at the first visit. Talk to the council and to two or three of the agencies above before you decide.

Sources: Why foster with us (London Borough of Barking and Dagenham); Fostering FAQs (London Borough of Barking and Dagenham); Fostering (London Borough of Barking and Dagenham); Fostering Barking and Dagenham; Local Community Fostering privacy notice (Waltham Forest Council).
