Fostering in Torbay

Children in care from Torbay are looked after by Torbay Council's own fostering service and by the independent agencies shown above. The council says every penny given to its fostering service goes on outcomes for the children in its care, and it looks for carers for children of every age from birth to 18. It also says that nearly 40% of Torbay's looked after children live more than 20 miles from home, against a national average of 13%, which is why it wants more carers in Torquay, Paignton and Brixham themselves.

Torbay's carers take emergency, short-term, long-term, respite, parent and baby, and family and friends placements, and brothers and sisters can stay together with one carer into adulthood. You need to be over 21, and there is no upper age limit. You can be single, married or divorced, of any sexual orientation, and own your home or rent somewhere stable; experience of looking after children matters more than being a parent.

The council describes a dedicated social worker, a mentor, an out-of-hours line, a local carers' forum, family events, local training and therapy support for children. It publishes weekly payments by age and carer level on its fostering support page, and says plans to raise them were recently approved, so check the current table with the team.

The council's route has five steps: a phone call, a home visit, a two-day Skills for Fostering course, an application with your own social worker, and a report to the fostering panel. It says approval usually takes around six months from your first enquiry, and is then reviewed every year. Our Devon and Plymouth pages cover the neighbouring areas. Speak to Torbay's team and to two or three of the services above before you decide.

Sources: Torbay Council: fostering in Torbay; Torbay Council: what we offer; Torbay Council: who can foster; Torbay Council: fostering support; Torbay Council: the process; Fostering Today: fostering agencies in Devon; Fostering Today: fostering agencies in Plymouth.
