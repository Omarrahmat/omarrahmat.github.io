Fostering in East London

East London is covered by several borough fostering services rather than one council. Since May 2024 six boroughs in north-east London, Barking and Dagenham, Havering, Newham, Redbridge, Tower Hamlets and Waltham Forest, have recruited together as Local Community Fostering. It acts as a single front door: it takes first enquiries and does the early screening, and your application is then assessed by the borough you choose. Hackney, to the west, works with its north London neighbours instead. The independent agencies shown above also approve and support carers across the area.

The need is real. When Local Community Fostering launched, Havering said more than 9,700 children in London needed a foster place, while approved fostering households in the capital had fallen from 3,685 in 2014 to 2,560 at the end of March 2023. It also said about seven in ten people who contact a council about fostering drop out before finishing, which the partnership set out to change.

Support between carers is a strength here. Barking and Dagenham has eight Mockingbird constellations, more than any other London borough, in which a hub carer with spare rooms offers short breaks, mentoring and social events to a group of local foster families. The borough says carers can also take over 60 training courses a year and have a buddy in their first year.

Every fostering service in London must pay at least the national minimum allowance, which for 2026-27 is £205 a week for a child aged 0 to 2, rising to £309 for a young person of 16 or 17; most services add a fee for the carer. Speak to your own borough and to two or three of the agencies above before you decide.
