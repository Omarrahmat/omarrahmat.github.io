Fostering in Southwark

Southwark Council's own fostering service and the independent agencies that list the borough as an area they cover are shown above. The council's statement of purpose for 2025 to 26 says it always tries to place its children with Southwark's own approved carers first, and where that is not possible they may go to carers approved and supported by independent fostering agencies, arranged by its placements team. Fostering for the council means caring for Southwark's children with its social workers; an agency assesses, trains and supervises you itself.

The council is candid that it needs more carers. It recruits according to the age, ethnicity and cultural needs of the children it looks after, says it sometimes has to place children some distance from the borough, and wants more specialist professional carers for young people with complex needs. When it last made a public appeal, in May 2024, it said about 25 local children were waiting for foster carers and that carer numbers were falling as many retired each year. Its own carers can tell it whether they would prefer teenagers, children with special needs or young babies.

If you apply to the council, it aims to finish the assessment within six months of allocating an assessor, with six or seven home visits over about five months, a three-day Skills to Foster preparation course and a decision at the Southwark Fostering Panel, which then sees each carer every three years. After approval a supervising social worker visits at least every six weeks, more often when a child first arrives, and carers have out-of-hours advice, support groups and paid membership of The Fostering Network. The council pays an age-related weekly allowance and fees on three levels, a setting-up grant and extra payments for holidays and festivals, and offers full Council Tax relief, but its pages give no amounts, so ask it and the agencies above for their figures.

There are also ways to help without fostering full time. Family Link offers short breaks for disabled children, from a weekend a month; the Weekenders programme, run with Now Foster, matches carers with a young person for one weekend a month; and supported lodgings hosts, who need a spare room but do not have to be foster carers, give a home to care leavers aged 16 to 21. Parent and child fostering is offered too.

Talk to the council and two or three of the services above, and see the Lambeth, Lewisham and Bromley pages, or London as a whole, if you live near the borough's edge.
