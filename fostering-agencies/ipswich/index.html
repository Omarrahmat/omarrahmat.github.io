Fostering in Ipswich

Ipswich is part of Suffolk, so children in care here are looked after by Suffolk County Council's fostering service, whose offices are at Endeavour House in the town, and by independent agencies, including those shown above. When Suffolk joined ten other councils in May 2024 to launch Foster East, a shared recruitment drive for the East of England, it said more than 900 children and young people in the county were in care, from babies to 18-year-olds.

The council says its most urgent need is for carers of teenagers, brothers and sisters, children with complex needs, parents with their babies, and young people who arrived in the UK alone. It also looks for LINK carers, who give disabled children short stays in their own homes, and short-break carers who give other foster families a rest.

You need to be 21 or over, with a bedroom the child does not share. There is no upper age limit, you do not need to own your home or be married, and LGBTQ+ applicants are welcome. Every fostering household must be smoke-free. Some experience of looking after children, such as bringing up your own or regular babysitting, is expected.

Suffolk pays its carers on a five-level scheme. For 2026-27 the weekly child allowance is £187 for a child aged up to 4, rising to £274 for 16 and 17-year-olds, and from Level 2 a weekly fee is paid on top, from £145 up to £524 at Level 5 for an older teenager. Our Suffolk pay page has the full table. Carers also get a dedicated social worker, a therapeutic team, an out-of-hours helpline, a Mockingbird hub and local support groups.

Suffolk's enquiries go through Foster East. Speak to the council and to two or three of the agencies above, and compare the support and payments each offers before you decide.
