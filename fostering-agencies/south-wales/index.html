Fostering in South Wales

South Wales is not one council area. It takes in Cardiff, Newport, Swansea and the Valleys, from Monmouthshire and Torfaen in the east to Neath Port Talbot in the west. Each council runs its own fostering team under the Foster Wales name, such as Foster Wales Cardiff, Foster Wales Caerphilly or Foster Wales Rhondda Cynon Taff, and independent agencies such as the services shown above also take carers here.

Foster Wales is the national network of the 22 local authority fostering teams in Wales, all of them not for profit. When it launched, the councils said Wales needed around 550 new foster carers every year. In June 2026 the Welsh Government said all 22 teams were working to a shared plan for 2026 to 2028.

Wales is also changing who can provide foster care. Since 1 April 2026 no new profit-making fostering provider can register in Wales; new services must be run by a council or by a not-for-profit body approved by the Welsh Government. Existing profit-making providers may carry on, but from 1 April 2027 they cannot approve new foster carers unless they have become not for profit. If you are looking at an agency, ask how it is set up and what this means for its carers.

Foster Wales's own guide to pay puts the weekly allowance in Wales for 2026-27 at usually around £229 for children under four, rising to £260 for young people over 16. Services can pay more, and most add a fee for the carer, so ask each one for its full figures.

For local detail, see our pages for Cardiff, Newport, Swansea, Bridgend, Caerphilly, Rhondda Cynon Taf and Monmouthshire, and our Wales page, then talk to your council's Foster Wales team and to two or three of the services above.
