Fostering in Croydon

The fostering services shown above all list Croydon as an area they cover, and the council runs its own service too, Foster Croydon. Caring for the council means taking Croydon's own children with its social workers alongside you; an independent agency does its own assessing, training and supervision, and councils, Croydon among them, ask agencies for homes when their own carers are full.

Croydon uses both. Its market position statement for 2025 to 2027 records that in June 2025, 189 of the borough's fostered children (45%) lived with independent agency carers and 227 (55%) with other foster carers, and among the carers it most needs it lists people who live in Croydon itself.

The fostering service's annual report for 2025-26 shows interest growing but carers also stepping back. Croydon had 152 approved fostering households on 31 March 2026, up from 139 a year before, and answered 1,079 requests for information against a target of 300. Eleven new households were approved against a target of 15, and ten were still being assessed at the year end. Over the same year 31 households came off the register, 16 of them family and friends carers, and twelve of the carers who left were over 60, which is why new carers matter. The report asks for more homes for teenagers, large sibling groups, unaccompanied minors, young people at risk of exploitation and some disabled children, describes new skilled, advanced and specialist pathways, and says Croydon needs to build care for emergencies.

Carers are not left alone. The council has opened three Horizons Hubs, each led by an experienced foster carer, offering peer support, friendships for the children, planned sleepovers for a break and help in a crisis, after a pilot with six foster families.

Croydon says it has become one of the most competitive and supportive councils in the south of England after its first pay review since 2022. Our Croydon allowance page sets out the scale: a weekly allowance for each child from £208 for under-fives to £309 for 16 and 17-year-olds, plus a weekly skills fee of £325 at entry level, £450 at the advanced level and £600 for specialist roles, so a newly approved carer receives £533 to £634 a week for one child. Carers also get Council Tax reimbursement, a £1,500 retention bonus each year after three years, and a £1,500 welcome payment if they transfer in. Talk to the council and two or three of the agencies above before choosing, and see the Bromley, Sutton, Merton and Lambeth pages, or London as a whole, if you live near the borough's edge.
