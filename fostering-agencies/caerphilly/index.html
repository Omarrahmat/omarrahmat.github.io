Fostering in Caerphilly

In Caerphilly county borough, the council's fostering service works as Foster Wales Caerphilly, part of the not-for-profit network of the 22 Welsh council fostering services, from Tredomen House in Ystrad Mynach. Children from Caerphilly, Blackwood, Bargoed, Risca and Ystrad Mynach are looked after by its carers and by the services shown above.

Foster Wales Caerphilly offers short-term and long-term fostering, short breaks, parent and child placements, and therapeutic fostering for children with more complex emotional or behavioural needs, backed by its own specialist support team. It also runs supported lodgings, where you give a room and guidance to a young person aged 16 to 21 as a lodger rather than as a registered foster carer.

Its route starts with an enquiry and a home visit (or a video call), then a few days of Skills to Foster preparation training, an assessment by specialist social workers, and Caerphilly's fostering panel. Once you are approved you sign a foster care agreement. The service describes training, ongoing support and financial allowances for its carers.

Foster Wales's national guide to foster carer pay sets out the minimum weekly allowances for 2026-27, from around £229 a week for children under four up to £260 a week for young people over 16, with any fee for the carer's skills paid on top. Ask each service exactly what it pays, as amounts above the minimum vary.

Our Wales page explains the national picture, including the rules that are moving children's care in Wales to councils and not-for-profit providers, and our Newport and Cardiff pages cover the neighbouring areas. Talk to Foster Wales Caerphilly and to one or two of the services above before you choose.
