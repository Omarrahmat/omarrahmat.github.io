Fostering in Somerset

Children in care in Somerset are looked after by Somerset Council's own service, Fostering in Somerset, and by the many independent agencies and charities listed on this page. In May 2026 the council said there were more than 650 children in its care and that it needed more local foster carers so that children can stay close to their schools, friends and communities. A year earlier it said that more than 60 per cent of the children in its care were aged 11 to 18, and that it had fewer than 150 fostering households of its own, so carers for teenagers are especially needed.

The council has a particular need for full-time carers who can offer a long-term home, but there are other ways to help: short-term and emergency care, occasional weekends or school holidays, and placements that support a parent with their young child. Carers need to be 21 or over and have a spare room; no experience is needed. The council provides training and pays its carers weekly.

Fostering in Somerset has held informal drop-in sessions around the county, most recently in Bridgwater, Taunton, Wells and Shepton Mallet, so people in those towns can meet the team and current carers before deciding. As agencies set their own fees and support, it is worth speaking to the council and two or three of the agencies below, and asking each about training, out-of-hours help and what they pay.

Sources: Take your first step into fostering at free events this May (Somerset Council, May 2026); Foster couple highlight urgent need for homes this Foster Care Fortnight (Bridgwater Town Council, 15 May 2025); Curious about fostering? Share a Cuppa with a Carer this November (Somerset Council, October 2025).
