Fostering in Luton

Nine fostering services list Luton as an area they cover: Luton Borough Council's own fostering service and eight independent agencies, all shown above. Fostering for the council means caring for Luton's own children, with the council's social workers supporting you; an independent agency carries out your assessment, approves and supervises you itself, and looks after children placed with it by councils.

Luton Borough Council said in March 2025 that it looks after more than 300 children and young people, and that many of them live outside the town because there are not enough fostering families locally. Its sufficiency strategy for 2025 to 2030 puts recruiting and keeping its own foster carers first. It records that 26.2% of placements in March 2025 were more than 20 miles away and aims to bring that under 18% within three years, and it plans to recruit 24 new council fostering households between April 2025 and March 2027. It wants more homes for older teenagers and for children with complex needs, and a group of carers that reflects the town's mix of cultures, ethnic backgrounds and faiths, so that a child's religion and language can be supported at home.

The council pays a weekly allowance for each child, from £189 for babies to £288 for 16 and 17-year-olds, and a weekly skills fee per child that rises with experience, from £145.90 in a carer's first year with Luton to £259.13 after five years. Birthday, festive and holiday allowances are paid on top, along with a £1,000 contribution towards Council Tax. Our Luton allowance page has the full scale.

The council says the whole process usually takes around six months, and it aims to complete the assessment within 16 weeks once it begins. You start with an expression of interest through Foster East, then an information event, a conversation with the team and a home visit, followed by checks and references and a social worker's assessment, which in Luton can run side by side. Fostering panel then makes a recommendation, which a senior manager confirms, and you are handed over to a supervising social worker. Preparation training, Skills to Foster, is six weekly three-hour sessions, with first aid and safeguarding training as well. A criminal conviction or caution does not automatically rule you out.

Speak to the council and to two or three of the agencies above to compare support and pay. If you live near the edge of the town, the pages for Bedfordshire, Hertfordshire and Milton Keynes list more services.

Sources: Strategic sufficiency strategy 2025 to 2030 (Luton Borough Council); Sufficiency strategic plan (Luton Borough Council); More foster carers from diverse backgrounds needed as Council has over 300 children in care (Luton Borough Council, March 2025); How to become a foster carer (Luton Borough Council); Luton foster carer allowance (Fostering Today).
