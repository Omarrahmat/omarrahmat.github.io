Fostering in Hartlepool

In Hartlepool, children who cannot live at home are cared for by Hartlepool Borough Council's own fostering service and by independent providers such as the services shown on this page. The council supports children from birth to 18 and says it gives priority to placing Hartlepool children with its own carers, so that more of them can stay close to the town they know. It also belongs to Foster with North East, a regional partnership of councils backed by the Department for Education that offers a single starting point for anyone thinking about fostering.

The council has said it urgently needs more foster homes for the children and young people in its care, particularly those aged 12 and over, and that it is looking for carers for both short-term and long-term placements.

Each council carer has a dedicated supervising social worker, met at least every four weeks and more often when needed, with out-of-hours help from the Emergency Duty Team. The council also describes a mentoring scheme for new carers, monthly support evenings and coffee mornings, tailored training, and notes that the region has signed up to the Fostering Network's Mockingbird programme. Payments come in two parts: a weekly allowance based on the child's age and a weekly fee set by the council's payment for skills model, which depends on the carer's skill level.

If you are ready to find out more, you can make a first enquiry through Foster with North East or the council's fostering pages. It is worth comparing what the council offers with two or three of the services on this page, asking each about training, day-to-day support and how their payments work, so you can choose the fit that feels right for your household.
