Fostering in St Helens

In St Helens, children in care are looked after by foster carers approved by St Helens Borough Council or by independent fostering agencies such as those listed on this page. In September 2025 the council said more than 430 children were in its care, and that it most needed carers for brothers and sisters who want to stay together, for older children and for children with additional needs. The council recruits through Foster4, a partnership of nine councils across Merseyside and Cheshire.

The council's 2025-26 statement of purpose describes short-term and long-term fostering, fostering for adoption, kinship care for relatives and friends, and specialist care for older children moving on from residential homes, as well as a Mockingbird constellation. Support set out there includes mentoring by experienced carers, support groups, forums with senior managers, membership of The Fostering Network and access to specialist nurses for children in care. New carers complete the national training standards in their first year.

For specialist carers, who look after children with complex needs and must be available full time, the council advertises a professional fee of up to £1,000 a week, with round-the-clock support. Applicants need to live in St Helens or within an hour's drive.

Anyone aged 21 or over who can offer a safe, caring home can apply. Talk to the council and to two or three of the agencies below, and compare what each offers in training, support and pay.

Sources: Fostering roadshow heads for St Helens this October (St Helens Borough Council, 30 September 2025); St Helens Fostering Service Statement of Purpose 2025/2026; Specialist Foster Carers (St Helens Borough Council).
