Fostering in Belfast

In Northern Ireland, the five Health and Social Care Trusts are responsible for children in care, and in Belfast that is the Belfast Health and Social Care Trust. Its fostering service is shown above with the independent agencies that list Belfast as an area they cover. HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care supports the Trusts' fostering services, and carers are needed for emergency, short-term, longer-term, kinship and short break fostering.

According to nidirect, there is no upper age limit for foster carers in Northern Ireland, and you can apply whether you are single or in a couple, have children of your own or not, are working or not, and own or rent your home. The assessment begins with training and a written application, then a GP medical, AccessNI and social services checks, references and a social worker's assessment of you and your family, before a fostering panel makes its recommendation.

Approved carers in the Belfast area are paid an allowance for each child every 28 days, with extra for birthdays and Christmas and an additional payment where a child has additional needs. Each carer has a supervising social worker, out-of-hours help comes from the Regional Emergency Social Work Service, and membership of The Fostering Network is provided. Belfast has its own learning and development programme for September 2026 to June 2027, a monthly support and development group and a monthly group for kinship carers.

Speak to the Trust and to two or three of the services above, and ask each how it trains and supports its carers before you choose.

Sources: Foster care (Belfast Health and Social Care Trust); Becoming a foster or kinship foster carer (nidirect); Foster carer support (HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care).
