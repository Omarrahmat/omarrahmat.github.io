Fostering in Shropshire

Children in care across Shropshire, from Shrewsbury and Oswestry to Ludlow, Market Drayton and Whitchurch, are looked after by Shropshire Fostering, the council's own not-for-profit service, and by the independent agencies listed on this page. In June 2025 the council said it was caring for more than 700 local children, that it wanted to keep brothers and sisters together in the county wherever possible, and that it needed more foster carers. It has also said that many children needing care are placed outside Shropshire, which is why it wants more local homes.

The need is wide. In December 2025 Shropshire Fostering appealed for carers for babies, alongside older children and teenagers, with placements lasting from a few weeks to around two years while plans are made for a child's future. It also offers emergency, short-term and long-term fostering and supported lodgings for young people moving towards independence.

Carers need to be 21 or over; you do not need to be in a relationship or own your home, and some carers keep working full-time. Shropshire Fostering says new carers can receive up to £45,000 a year for caring for one child, paid weekly, with more for experienced carers and for more than one child, and it guarantees payments between placements. Support includes the Skills to Foster course, a supervising social worker, a 24-hour duty line, a mentor scheme and local carer groups. Ofsted rated Shropshire Council's children's services outstanding in 2025.

As agencies set their own fees and support, it is worth speaking to Shropshire Fostering and two or three of the services below before you decide.

Sources: A night of gratitude: Shropshire to celebrate foster carers at July event (Shropshire Council, 17 June 2025); Shropshire Fostering urges families to open homes to babies (Shropshire Star, 11 December 2025); Shropshire Fostering celebrates outstanding contribution to children's services (Shropshire Star, 3 October 2025); Foster carers (Shropshire Council careers).
