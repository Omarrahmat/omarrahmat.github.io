Fostering in Greater Manchester

Children in care in Greater Manchester are looked after by the city region's ten councils and by the independent fostering agencies listed on this page. The ten councils recruit together under one banner, Foster for Greater Manchester, a partnership run with the Greater Manchester Combined Authority. Carers still foster with their own council, which provides local training, specialist social workers and support groups.

In October 2023 the lead councillors for children's services in all ten boroughs signed the Greater Manchester Fostering Promise, a shared commitment to clear and honest support for foster carers and a relationship built on trust. Greater Manchester was also chosen as a pathfinder area for the national fostering reforms. An earlier campaign, Fostering Unfiltered, used existing local carers to show everyday family life in fostering, and the combined authority described the city region's community of carers as nearly 1,500 strong.

The partnership says people of any age, sexuality, gender, marital status, employment status, ethnicity, religion and background can make good foster carers. You need to be over 21, have a suitable home that you own or rent securely, good spoken and written English, and be open about any criminal record. Enquiries get a reply within one working day.

Look at the page for your own borough too, such as Manchester, Salford, Stockport or Wigan. Speak to your council and to two or three of the agencies below, and ask each about the children they most need homes for, their training and support, and what they pay, before you decide.

Sources: Foster for Greater Manchester (Greater Manchester Combined Authority); Fostering Promise launches first ever Greater Manchester Fostering Fortnight (GMCA, 18 October 2023); Greater Manchester residents urged to consider fostering as part of new campaign (GMCA, 27 June 2023).
