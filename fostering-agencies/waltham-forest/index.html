Fostering in Waltham Forest

Waltham Forest Council is looking for foster carers living in and around the borough to give a family home to children who cannot live with their own families, whether for a short emergency stay or for much longer. Its own fostering service is shown above with the independent agencies that list Waltham Forest as an area they cover.

First enquiries go through Local Community Fostering, a hub the council shares with Barking and Dagenham, Havering, Newham, Redbridge and Tower Hamlets. The hub has an initial chat with you and does the first screening, then passes your application to the council, whose fostering team carries out a two-stage assessment: background checks first, then a fuller look at your suitability, before the fostering and adoption panel.

The council describes fostering as a paid professional role with training and support, working alongside social workers and teachers. It welcomes people of any age, ethnicity, religion, sexuality, marital status or employment status, and it is open to experienced carers who want to move across from an independent agency. You will need a spare bedroom, although a baby can share your room until the age of two if there is space for a cot. The council's fostering privacy notice also says its foster carers receive a council tax exemption each year.

If you live in Waltham Forest, talk to the council and to two or three of the agencies above, and ask each what it pays, what training it gives and how it supports carers before you decide.

Sources: Fostering in Waltham Forest (London Borough of Waltham Forest); Local Community Fostering privacy notice (Waltham Forest Council); Adoption and fostering service privacy notice (Waltham Forest Council).
