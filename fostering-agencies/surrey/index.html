Fostering in Surrey

Children in care in Surrey are looked after by Surrey County Council's fostering service and by the independent fostering agencies listed below. The council says you will never be expected to foster alone, and its assessment is designed both to prepare you and to make sure children are placed with families who can offer stability and safety.

Surrey's support for its own carers is set out in detail. Each household has a supervising social worker who visits at least every six weeks, an experienced foster carer as a buddy, a fostering support worker, 24-hour emergency support and therapeutic help when fostering gets hard. There are online and in-person support groups, including holiday groups for carers' own children, and the Surrey Foster Care Association offers support, advice and advocacy.

The council offers more than 200 training courses, with the chance to gain accredited qualifications. Its carers also get Fostering Network and National Association of Therapeutic Parents membership, and the council takes part in the Mockingbird Family Model. It pays an allowance for each child but does not list the amounts on its fostering pages, so ask for the current rates.

If you already foster, the council has a route for transferring from another agency. Agencies set their own fees and support, so speak to the council and two or three of the agencies on this page and compare what each offers before you decide.

Sources: Why foster with us? (Surrey County Council); How to become a foster carer (Surrey County Council).
