Fostering in Redbridge

Redbridge Council's fostering service is part of Local Community Fostering, a partnership with Havering, Tower Hamlets and Waltham Forest councils. The council is keen to find carers who can keep brothers and sisters together, carers for teenagers, shared care carers for disabled children, parent and child carers, and specialist carers for young people moving from residential care into a family home. Placements can last from a single night to beyond the age of 18, and include short-term, long-term, emergency and short break fostering.

You need to be 21 or over, with no upper age limit if you are fit and well, and have a spare bedroom, although a baby under three can sleep in a cot in your room. Renters need their landlord's permission, and the council cannot accept applicants living in temporary accommodation or with lodgers. You do not need to be married or a British citizen. After a phone call and a home visit of about two hours, you attend a two-day Skills to Foster course; the assessment then usually takes three to six months, with eight to ten visits from a social worker, before the fostering panel.

The council's fostering page lists weekly payments of between £376.96 and £560.48 per child depending on the child's age, with higher rates for teenagers, between £656.96 and £840.48 for children who need a high level of care, and between £806.96 and £1,140.48 for young people moving from residential care. It also lists a council tax discount of 60%, rising to 80% where occupancy conditions are met, a training bonus of up to £200 a year, and extra payments for birthdays, festivals and holidays.

Support includes a supervising social worker, a fostering buddy in your first year, monthly peer groups, a group for male foster carers, a carer-run Foster Carers Association, access to a clinical psychologist and the Mockingbird family model. Compare the council with two or three of the agencies shown above before you decide.

Sources: Fostering (Redbridge Council).
