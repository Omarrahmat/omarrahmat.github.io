Fostering in South Yorkshire

South Yorkshire is a region rather than a single council. Fostering for children in care here is run by four councils, Barnsley, Doncaster, Rotherham and Sheffield, each with its own fostering service, and independent agencies that cover the county are shown above. Each council area has its own page on this site: Fostering agencies in Barnsley, Doncaster, Rotherham and Sheffield.

Fostering Barnsley offers mentoring for every carer, Mockingbird hubs, a foster carers' association, and a pilot scheme for specialist carers who give full-time therapeutic care to children with complex emotional needs. Foster for Doncaster runs five Mockingbird hubs, support groups from babies to teenagers, a group for new carers and support around the clock, and approved carers moving from another service can transfer without a full new assessment. Fostering Rotherham describes itself as a not-for-profit council service with a dedicated social worker, a buddy system, three Mockingbird hubs with respite and free specialist support groups.

Sheffield's fostering service was rated good by Ofsted in September 2023, with the experiences and progress of children in its care judged outstanding. Sheffield asks that carers are at least 21 with a spare bedroom and live in the UK full time. It says there is no upper age limit, you do not need to own your home, drive or hold qualifications, and single people can foster with a support network around them.

Every service in England must pay at least the national minimum allowance. For 2026-27, outside London and the South East, that is £176 a week for a child aged 0 to 2, £182 for ages 3 to 4, £201 for ages 5 to 10, £227 for ages 11 to 15 and £267 for ages 16 to 17. Many services pay more, including a fee for the carer, so compare what each offers.

Wherever you live in South Yorkshire, speak to two or three of the services above, ask about training, support and the children they most need homes for, and go to an information session before you decide.
