Fostering in Brighton and Hove

Brighton & Hove City Council runs its own not-for-profit fostering service, and the services that list Brighton and Hove among their areas are shown above. The council tries to place children with its own carers first so they can stay near family, friends and school. Many of its carers came from independent agencies: it says a transfer usually takes four to five months, with 28 days' written notice to your current service.

The council's fostering booklet, updated in December 2025, says the children who need homes range from newborn babies to 18-year-olds, and many are part of a group of brothers and sisters. The city is short of carers from Black, Asian and Global Majority backgrounds who share the heritage of children coming into care; carers looking after a child from a different background get extra training, a cultural identity support group and an experienced buddy. When councillors reviewed fostering pay in June 2023, the report named teenagers and young people aged 16 and over as priorities and set an aim of recruiting 22 carers a year.

You need a spare bedroom, real experience of looking after children through family, work or volunteering, good health and people around you for support, and you must live within 25 miles of the city; the same 2023 report said over a third of the council's carers lived in West Sussex. Besides short-term, long-term, respite and parent and child care, the council offers Fostering Plus for young people moving out of residential care, care for young people seeking asylum and supported lodgings for care leavers. It started Mockingbird in March 2025 and now has two groups of linked households.

Since 2024 the council has been one of 20 councils in Local Authority Fostering South East, a hub funded by the Department for Education that gives people one place to start an enquiry. At its launch the partners said the south east had over 11,000 children in care and fewer than 3,000 council-approved foster carers.

Becoming a council carer takes about six months, from an information event and home visit through checks, the Skills to Foster course and a Form F assessment to panel. Carers get a supervising social worker, support around the clock, a buddy for the first six to twelve months, activities for the children, Fostering Network membership and a Blue Light Card. Pay is a weekly fee plus an allowance for the child; we have not published the council's rates, so compare it with the services above. The East Sussex and West Sussex pages list more services nearby.

Sources: Fostering in Brighton & Hove (Brighton & Hove City Council); Fostering with Brighton & Hove City Council, booklet updated December 2025; Black, Asian and Global Majority foster carers (Brighton & Hove City Council); Review of Fostering Allowances, Children, Families & Schools Committee, 12 June 2023 (Brighton & Hove City Council); South east councils join forces to recruit more foster carers (Brighton & Hove City Council, 2024).
