Fostering in Chorley

In Chorley, fostering for children in care is run by Lancashire County Council, which describes its service as the not-for-profit local authority fostering service for the county, alongside the independent agencies shown above. The council aims to match local children with its own carers so they can stay near family, friends and school, and it holds talk-to-the-team events in the Chorley and West Lancashire area.

The county's fostering statement of purpose says its recruitment team focuses on brothers and sisters, teenagers and older children, parents with their children, unaccompanied asylum-seeking children and children with additional needs. The service offers short-term, long-term, emergency, short-break and parent and child homes, and it aims to complete assessments within four months. Every new applicant goes on a three-day preparation course called Skills to Foster.

Lancashire's carers get a dedicated supervising social worker, local offices, help at any hour and an in-house therapeutic support team, with training that starts before approval and a mentor during the first year through its Fostering Academy. The council speaks of generous allowances, with extra payments for siblings, children with additional needs and parent and child placements; it lists the amounts on its own website.

The county says no formal experience or qualifications are needed: applicants should be over 21, live in or near Lancashire and have a spare room or plans for one. Speaking to the council and to two or three of the agencies above is the best way to compare support, training and pay.

Sources: Foster for Lancashire (Lancashire County Council); Fostering statement of purpose 2023-26 (Lancashire County Council); Plans to boost crucial support for foster carers in pipeline (Lancashire County Council, 4 December 2024); Community events (Lancashire County Council fostering).
