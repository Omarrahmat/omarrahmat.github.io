Fostering in North Yorkshire

North Yorkshire Council runs its own fostering service, and children from the county also live with independent agencies such as the services shown above. The council says it has helped more than 400 children into loving homes through fostering, and that its children in care include more older children and fewer babies and toddlers.

That shapes what it asks for. As well as short-term, long-term and emergency fostering, it needs short-break carers for disabled children (at least three full days and nights a month) and is urgently seeking specialist carers for young people aged 16 to 18 with more complex needs. For that role it advertises fees and allowances of more than £840 a week. North Yorkshire also runs No Wrong Door, hubs that combine residential care with fostering for young people in or on the edge of care; the council says these roles can suit people who work full or part time.

The council publishes a standard weekly fostering allowance of £182.10 for children aged up to four, £207.47 for five to 10, £258.12 for 11 to 15 and £314.02 for 16 and over, with extra payments for birthdays, holidays and festivals and help with costs such as uniform, travel and school trips. It says plainly that there is no set foster fee and that, as a not-for-profit service, it may not match every agency's package.

Becoming a carer takes up to six months: an enquiry, a virtual visit and Skills to Foster training, a formal application, checks, assessment with a named social worker, then panel. Ask the council and the agencies above how they would support you in your part of the county.

Sources: Fostering North Yorkshire (North Yorkshire Council); Types of fostering (North Yorkshire Council); Fostering support and payments (North Yorkshire Council); Steps to fostering (North Yorkshire Council); Over 400 children supported into loving homes through fostering (Your North Yorkshire, May 2026).
