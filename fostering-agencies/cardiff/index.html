Fostering in Cardiff

Five fostering services list Cardiff as an area they cover: the council's own service, Foster Wales Cardiff, and four independent organisations, all shown above. Foster Wales Cardiff is one of the 22 local authority fostering services in the national Foster Wales network, which runs on a not-for-profit basis. If you foster with the council you care for children it is legally responsible for, with a dedicated social worker of your own; an independent service assesses and supports you itself and looks after children placed with it by councils.

Fostering in Wales is changing. The Welsh Government has committed to removing private profit from the care of looked-after children, and Cardiff Council has said that by 2027 that care will be provided by public sector, charitable or not-for-profit organisations. When you compare services, it is worth asking each one how it is set up and how the change affects the carers it supports.

The need is large. In January 2024 the council said 400 children in Cardiff were living in foster care, while 105 foster carers were approved with Foster Wales Cardiff, and that across Wales more than 7,000 children were in care against only 3,800 foster families. Foster Wales set a goal of more than 800 new foster families by 2026. The council's own site speaks of a critical shortage of carers and says hundreds of new foster families are needed across the city each year, for babies and toddlers, teenagers, brothers and sisters, and young parents with their babies.

Parent and child fostering is one of the roles Foster Wales Cardiff recruits for: a parent and baby usually stay for around 12 weeks, sometimes longer, and the carer needs a spare bedroom and must be at home throughout. Council carers are offered round-the-clock support, peer support events, training and social events with other local fostering families, and Foster Wales lists extras such as a Blue Light Card, free Cadw membership and membership of The Fostering Network.

We do not yet hold a council allowance page for Cardiff, because the council does not publish its rates by age band and year, so ask each service for its current figures in writing. Talk to Foster Wales Cardiff and to the agencies above, and see the pages for Newport, Bridgend, Swansea and Wales as a whole if you live outside the city.
