Fostering in Hounslow

Hounslow says it has more than 300 children and young people in care who cannot live with their own family, and that it wants children to stay living in the borough when they cannot stay with relatives. Its own service, Foster Hounslow, is part of Foster with West London, the regional service for eight west London councils, whose staff handle everything from a first enquiry to assessment. The independent agencies shown above also take enquiries from Hounslow, and councils turn to agencies when their own carers cannot offer a home.

The council particularly welcomes people interested in fostering teenagers and brothers and sisters together. It also gives a safe home to unaccompanied children seeking asylum, the largest numbers from Afghanistan and Albania, and asks carers for those children to take specialist training and learn about the child's culture. In November 2025 it launched a campaign, "Grow in new ways", aimed at professionals, couples and single people over 30 who may never have thought about fostering, because the number of foster carers across the UK is falling. In May 2026 it marked Foster Care Fortnight by celebrating its longest-serving active carers, who began fostering for Hounslow in the 1980s.

You need to be over 21, in good health, and live or work within reasonable reach of the borough so you can manage school runs, family time and health appointments. A spare room is needed for children over two. You can rent, own or live in council housing (a private landlord is asked to agree in writing), and you can be single, married or living with a partner, LGBT+ or disabled. No qualifications are needed, as the council trains you. Most people keep working, though someone must be at home full-time for a baby under two, and respite care suits less flexible hours.

The council describes nine stages to approval, including a telephone questionnaire, Skills to Foster training, checks and six personal references, six to eight assessment visits and a fostering panel, usually over four to six months, with a start-up grant towards essential furniture during the assessment. Approved carers get a supervising social worker, support around the clock, ongoing training, Council Tax refunded while a child is in their care, Fostering Network membership and a Fusion leisure pass.

Before you choose, compare what the council offers with two or three of the agencies above. If you live near the borough's edge, the Ealing, Brent and Harrow pages, or London as a whole, may help too.
