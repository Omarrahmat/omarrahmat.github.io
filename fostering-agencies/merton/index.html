Fostering in Merton

Merton Council's own fostering service is shown above, together with the independent agencies that list Merton as an area they cover. Fostering with the council means looking after children from the borough with its social workers behind you; an independent agency assesses, approves and supports its own carers. Merton says its own carers are the first port of call for its children, and only those it cannot place with them go to other agencies.

The council's sufficiency strategy, issued in February 2022, gives the clearest picture. Merton looked after 142 children on 31 March 2021, down from 154 a year earlier, and 85% of them lived in foster care, against 71% nationally. Just under half (46%) were with the council's own carers. It had 81 approved fostering households (71 mainstream and 10 family and friends carers) and had recruited 13 mainstream carers in the year, and only about a third of children were placed inside the borough. The strategy commits to more council foster homes, especially for unaccompanied young people aged 15 to 17, teenagers with complex needs, brothers and sisters, and parents with babies.

Merton says Ofsted rated its service Good with Outstanding features. Its carers get their own supervising social worker, a duty social worker out of hours and at weekends, training before approval and through the year, a buddy for newly approved carers, a Foster Carer Association that meets regularly, and a paid Fostering Network membership. The council describes its allowance as based on the child's age and needs but does not publish the figures there. It also uses the Mockingbird model, in which six to ten fostering homes form a group around a hub home with two spare beds for sleepovers and monthly get-togethers.

Talk to the council and two or three of the agencies before you decide, and ask each what it pays and how it supports carers. If you live near the borough's edges, the Croydon, Sutton, Wandsworth, Lambeth, South London and London pages list more services nearby.

Sources: Securing Sufficient Accommodation for Children in Care and Care-Experienced Young People, February 2022 (Merton Council); Why choose us? Fostering with Merton (Merton Council); Mockingbird Family Model (Merton Council).
