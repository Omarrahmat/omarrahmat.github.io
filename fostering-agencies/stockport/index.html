Fostering in Stockport

Stockport Council's own fostering service is a not-for-profit local authority service, and it says more than 300 children are in foster placements in the borough at any one time. Children who cannot live at home are cared for by council foster carers or by independent agencies such as those on this page, and the council stresses the value of children staying close to their schools and families.

A significant number of the young people who need foster care in Stockport are older children and teenagers, and the council wants carers from a range of backgrounds that reflect the young people in its care. Fostering can range from a few nights a week to a long-term home, and you need to be over 21.

Stockport publishes its weekly allowances for general fostering by age band: on its payments page (last updated in May 2026) they run from £366 a week for children up to four to £572 a week for 16 and 17-year-olds, with extra payments for birthdays, Christmas or festivals and holidays, and a one-off clothing payment. Specialist fostering and caring for disabled or autistic children have different rates. There is no payment when no child is placed.

Council foster families also get free Life Leisure membership and a Max Card for days out, along with local training and support groups. Whether you live in Bramhall, Cheadle, Marple or the town centre, ask the council and two or three of the agencies below how their pay and support compare.

Sources: Payments and allowances (Stockport Council); Fostering with us (Stockport Council); Fostering teenagers (Stockport Council); Stockport Council calls for more foster carers as Foster Care Fortnight launches.
