Fostering in West Sussex

West Sussex County Council runs its own not-for-profit fostering service. It says more than 850 children are in its care and that about two in three of them are aged 11 to 17, so carers for teenagers are especially needed. It also looks for carers for asylum-seeking children and for short breaks for children with additional needs. Children may also live with independent agencies such as those listed on this page.

There is no upper age limit, and you can be single, in a same-sex relationship, renting or owning, with pets or children of your own, as long as you have a spare room. The council's teenager campaign says carers receive up to £515.06 a week, depending on the child's age and the carer's skill level.

Carers have a supervising social worker with regular one-to-one supervision, training built around the secure base model, out-of-hours support, support groups, a counselling service, membership of The Fostering Network and a local foster carers' association. West Sussex is also part of Local Authority Fostering South East, a recruitment partnership of councils across the region.

Before deciding, compare what the council offers with two or three of the agencies below, and ask each about pay, training and support near you.

Sources: They need a chance to shine: foster a teenager (West Sussex County Council); WSCC Fostering Service (West Sussex Local Offer); West Sussex joins regional partnership to recruit more foster carers (West Sussex County Council).
