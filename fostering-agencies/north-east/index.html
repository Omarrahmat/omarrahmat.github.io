Fostering in North East

The North East covers Northumberland, Tyneside, Wearside, County Durham and the Tees Valley. Children in care across the region are looked after by twelve councils, each with its own fostering service, and by independent agencies such as the services shown above.

The twelve councils recruit together as Foster with North East: Darlington, Durham, Gateshead, Hartlepool, Middlesbrough, Newcastle, Northumberland, North Tyneside, Redcar and Cleveland, South Tyneside, Stockton-on-Tees and Sunderland. The hub, run by Together for Children on behalf of Sunderland, is backed by the Department for Education and is a single first stop for anyone thinking about fostering with a council. You are then supported through your application by your own council's fostering team, with help from experienced carers in the hub, and new carers can be paired with a buddy mentor. The councils say the region was the first in the UK to sign up in full to the Mockingbird programme.

Sunderland reported on the hub's first anniversary in October 2024 that more than 850 people had enquired, that applications were taking an average of 4.8 months to reach approval against a national average of six, and that 23.1 per cent of first enquiries had become applications, around double the national rate of 12 per cent.

Every fostering service in the North East must pay at least the government's minimum allowance for the rest of England. For 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027 that is £176 a week for a child aged 0 to 2, £182 for 3 to 4, £201 for 5 to 10, £227 for 11 to 15 and £267 for 16 to 17. Many services add a fee for the carer on top: our Newcastle and Northumberland pay pages show what those two councils pay.

For more local detail, see our pages for Newcastle, Sunderland, Gateshead, County Durham, Northumberland, Middlesbrough and Hartlepool, then talk to your council and to two or three of the services above.
