Fostering in Wales

Across Wales, children who cannot live at home are looked after by the 22 council fostering services, which work together under the national name Foster Wales, and by the charities and independent agencies listed on this page. In March 2025 Bridgend County Borough Council said Wales had more than 7,000 children in the care system but only about 3,800 foster families, and that Foster Wales aimed to recruit more than 800 new foster families by 2026.

Wales is changing who can provide foster care. Under the Health and Social Care (Wales) Act 2025, the Welsh Government says that from 1 April 2026 any new fostering provider registering in Wales must be not-for-profit, and existing profit-making providers cannot add new services. Care Inspectorate Wales checks that providers meet the rules, and the government says the changes are being phased in carefully so that no child's care is disrupted. Its stated aim is that, in future, care for looked after children will come only from councils, charities and other not-for-profit organisations.

For you as a prospective carer, the practical step is the same as anywhere: talk to your local Foster Wales service and to one or two of the other services below, and ask each about training, support at any hour, what they pay, and how the new rules affect them. Fostering services in Wales register with Care Inspectorate Wales.

Sources: Wales leading the way: profit removed from children's homes, fostering and secure accommodation from 1 April (Welsh Government, 10 March 2026); Removing profit from the care of children looked after newsletter: March 2026 (Welsh Government); Foster carers in Bridgend County Borough welcome plan to phase profit out of children's care (Bridgend County Borough Council, 4 March 2025); Foster Wales Carmarthenshire (Carmarthenshire County Council).
