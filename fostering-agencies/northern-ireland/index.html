Fostering in Northern Ireland

In Northern Ireland, children in care are the responsibility of five Health and Social Care Trusts: Belfast, Northern, South Eastern, Southern and Western. Each Trust runs its own fostering service, supported by HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care, which recruits carers in all five Trust areas. Independent fostering agencies, such as the services shown above, also take carers. HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care says 3,514 children and young people relied on foster care in Northern Ireland in March 2025.

Trust foster carers offer emergency, short-term, longer-term, kinship and short break care. According to nidirect, there is no upper age limit, and you can apply whether you are single or in a couple, have children or not, are working or not, and own or rent your home, whatever your race, religion or sexuality. You complete some training and an application, then have a GP medical, AccessNI and social services checks, references and a social worker's assessment of your family, before a fostering panel makes its recommendation.

Allowances for Trust carers are set across Northern Ireland, and The Fostering Network notes it is the only UK nation where every Trust carer, kinship carers included, receives the same rate. The Department of Health's scale from 1 April 2025 was £155 a week for children aged 0 to 4, £173 for 5 to 10, £194 for 11 to 15 and £228 for 16 and over, paid every four weeks. The Fostering Network reports that a 3% rise for 2026-27 was announced in May 2026, backdated to 1 April, taking the weekly minimum to between £160 and £235 by age. Agencies set their own pay, so ask each one.

Our Belfast page covers the Belfast Trust area. Talk to your local Trust and to two or three of the services above before you choose.

Sources: HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care; HSC NI Adoption and Foster Care: foster care; nidirect: becoming a foster or kinship foster carer; Department of Health: foster care allowances 2025-26 (scale rates from 1 April 2025); The Fostering Network: allowances; Fostering Today: fostering agencies in Belfast.
