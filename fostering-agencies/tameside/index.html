Fostering in Tameside

Families in Tameside can foster with the council's own service, which works under the Foster for Tameside name and takes enquiries through Foster for Greater Manchester, or with one of the independent agencies listed above. The council describes its service as not for profit, with its carers working directly alongside the borough's children's services.

The council says more foster carers are needed in the borough, and it recruits for short-term, long-term, specialist, emergency and respite care. To apply you need to be 21 or over and have a spare room, unless you are fostering a child under two. There is no upper age limit and no formal qualifications are needed, though experience of children, including relatives' or friends' children, helps. Single people, LGBT+ people, people with pets, people living in flats and people who do not drive can all apply. The main carer is usually expected to foster full time, but part-time or flexible work is talked through case by case.

Carers receive an allowance based on the child's age and their own skills, along with training (some of it online) and ongoing support. The council does not publish its rates on these pages, so ask for them when you enquire. It also runs Let's Talk Fostering information sessions with local carers, held at Portland Basin Museum in Ashton-under-Lyne and online during 2026, and has held a family fun day for carers and the children they look after.

The council aims to reply to online enquiries within two working days. Talk to the council and two or three of the services above before deciding, and see the Oldham, Stockport and Manchester pages if you live near the borough's edge.

Sources: Who can foster (Tameside Council); What fostering is (Tameside Council); Find out more about fostering at upcoming information sessions (Tameside Correspondent, 16 February 2026); Tameside foster carers enjoy Fostering Family Fun Day (Tameside Council, August 2024).
