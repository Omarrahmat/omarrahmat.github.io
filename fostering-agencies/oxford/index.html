Fostering in Oxford

Children in care who live in Oxford are looked after by Oxfordshire County Council, which runs fostering for the city as well as the rest of the county, and by independent agencies such as those shown above. The council says it wants children to stay in Oxfordshire, close to their families, friends and schools, and describes its own service as fully local and not for profit.

Since July 2024 the county council has been part of Local Authority Fostering South East, a regional hub for 20 councils set up as part of a Department for Education pilot. When it launched, the council said there were over 11,000 children in care across the South East and fewer than 3,000 council-approved foster carers, and that its aim was more stability for children by placing them locally with its own carers. Enquiries to the council now go through the hub's form.

On pay, the council says carers on its therapeutic fostering programme can receive up to £30,576 a year for caring for an 11-year-old, that it also makes skills payments, and that for most carers the payments are tax-free on what they receive. Carers also get a support team, local training, support groups and the Mockingbird family model, along with leisure centre discounts, Oxford United match tickets and trips run by the Oxfordshire Foster Care Association.

If you live in Oxford, speak to the council and to two or three of the agencies above, and compare training, support and payments for the children you could care for. The Oxfordshire page covers the wider county.

Sources: Fostering with Oxfordshire County Council; Oxfordshire County Council joins regional hub to recruit more foster carers (8 July 2024).
