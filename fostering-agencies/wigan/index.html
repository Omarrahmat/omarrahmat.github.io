Fostering in Wigan

Wigan Council looks after its children in care through its own fostering service, and some children from the borough live with independent agencies such as those shown above. The council's sufficiency strategy for 2025 to 2028 counted 741 children looked after on 31 March 2024, up from 679 a year earlier. By 31 March 2025, 72% were living in foster homes and 71% were placed inside the borough boundary, so most children can stay near their schools and families.

The same strategy names who needs homes most. Children aged 10 to 15 are the largest group in care, at 32%, and the council wants more carers who can offer a home to teenagers. Its fostering pages add brothers and sisters who should stay together and children with complex needs. The strategy also records that the number of independent agency fostering households used by Wigan rose by 35.6% over two years, and it sets out to match more children with the council's own carers, working with other Greater Manchester councils through the Foster for Greater Manchester recruitment hub.

Wigan publishes its rates. From 6 April 2026 its booklet sets weekly payments in tiers by the child's age: for a child aged 5 to 10, £328.39 for a newly approved carer and £414.20 for an experienced carer, and for a child aged 11 to 15, £371.36 and £467.61. A higher tier is paid to specialist carers looking after children with complex needs or moving from residential care. Our Wigan allowance page sets out the full table and the extra allowances.

The council's statement of purpose for 2025-26 aims to complete assessments within 20 weeks. It describes short and long-term, kinship, parent and child, sleepover and support care, four Mockingbird hubs where an experienced carer supports nearby families, supervising social worker visits every four to twelve weeks, a monthly peer support group and help from CAMHS and the Virtual School.

Fostering for the council means working directly with the team that holds responsibility for Wigan's children; an agency recruits and supports its own carers and is asked by councils to find homes. Speak to the council and two or three of the agencies above and compare pay, training and support.

Sources: Looked After Children Sufficiency Strategy 2025 - 2028 (Wigan Council); Fostering Statement of Purpose 2025/26 (Wigan Council); Why foster for Wigan?
