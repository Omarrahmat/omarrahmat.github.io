Fostering in Ealing

If you are searching for a fostering agency in Ealing, the services shown above all take carers from the borough, and the council runs its own service, Ealing Fostering Connections, which finds foster homes for Ealing children who cannot live with their own families. The difference matters: council carers look after the borough's own children with its social workers beside them, while an independent agency recruits, approves and supervises its carers itself and offers them to councils, Ealing included, when a home is needed.

Ealing's sufficiency strategy for 2025 to 2028 sets out the local picture. The borough looked after 280 children in September 2024, 8% fewer than a year earlier. In August 2024, 56 children were in the council's own foster homes and 98 in independent agency homes, and the number of approved or newly approved mainstream council households fell by 14% between 2020 and 2024. Location matters too: 61% of its in-house placements were inside the borough, against 11% of agency placements. Its recruitment advert asked in particular for carers for brothers and sisters of all ages, school-age children including teenagers, babies, parents with their child, and children with additional needs and disabilities.

Ealing is one of eight councils in Foster with West London. Brent's cabinet report of July 2026 explains that the partnership began in May 2024 as a shared front door for enquiries and marketing and is moving to assess and train all new carers for its member councils, and that its 41 approvals so far include 15 weekend-only respite carers recruited by Ealing. Enquiries from Ealing residents go through the partnership.

In October 2024 Ealing's cabinet agreed an 18.5% uplift in its foster carers' allowances along with a wider package. As the council described it, that package includes council tax exemption for eligible carers, free borough-wide parking in pay and display and residents' bays, discounted leisure, free green waste collection, in-house therapeutic support from a clinical psychology service, a dedicated out-of-hours team, peer support, the Mockingbird scheme and ongoing training. We do not yet have an Ealing allowance page, so ask the council and each agency for their current weekly rates in writing before you decide.

Speak to the council and two or three of the agencies above, and ask each how it supports carers of teenagers and sibling groups. Families near the borough's edges can also look at the Brent, Hounslow, Harrow and London pages.
