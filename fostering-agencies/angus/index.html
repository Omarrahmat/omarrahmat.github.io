Fostering in Angus

In Angus, children who cannot live with their own families are cared for by Angus Council's fostering and adoption service and by the independent agencies shown above. The council says it places Angus children with its own local carers first and turns to agencies when no suitable local carer is free, and that it urgently needs more foster carers. Its carers look after children from birth to 18, in interim, long-term, permanent and short-break care.

In June 2024 the council reported that the Care Inspectorate had rated its fostering and adoption service as very good. It currently runs a campaign aimed at people who have worked in health or the emergency services, featuring carers with backgrounds in ambulance work and nursing.

The council says a carer looking after one child can receive up to £26.5k in their first year. Pay is made up of a weekly allowance that depends on the child's age and a fostering fee at one of three levels, set by training and experience. Carers get a supervising social worker, an out-of-hours team, local training and regular breaks.

To foster with the council you need to be at least 21, have a spare bedroom (or suitable space for a baby) and live in a smoke-free home. The process starts with an enquiry or an information event, followed by a home visit and two or three days of preparation training, and usually takes around six months. Speaking to the council and to two or three of the agencies above is the best way to compare training, support and pay.

Sources: Fostering (Angus Council); Why foster with us (Angus Council); How to become a foster carer (Angus Council); Angus Council Fostering and Adoption Service rated 'Very Good' by Care Inspectorate (17 June 2024); Blue light campaign (Angus Council).
