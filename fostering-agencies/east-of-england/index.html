Fostering in East of England

The East of England is a region, not a single council. It takes in Norfolk, Suffolk, Essex, Hertfordshire, Cambridgeshire and Bedfordshire, along with Peterborough, Luton, Southend-on-Sea and Thurrock. Children in care are looked after by each of those councils' fostering services and by independent agencies such as the services shown above, many of which take carers from across the region.

Eleven councils now recruit together through Foster East, which opened on 29 May 2024 with Department for Education funding: Bedford Borough, Cambridgeshire, Central Bedfordshire, Essex, Hertfordshire, Luton, Norfolk, Peterborough, Southend-on-Sea, Suffolk and Thurrock. Its advisers take calls from 8am to 8pm every day, you can talk to someone who already fosters, and your enquiry is passed to your own council. At the launch the councils said more than 7,000 children were in care across the region, with around 150 more coming into care each month, and Suffolk said it particularly needed carers for children aged 11 and over. Carers already with an agency can also transfer to their council through the hub.

Every fostering service must pay at least the government's minimum allowance. For 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027 the rest of England rates run from £176 a week for a baby to £267 for a 16 or 17 year old, and the higher South East rates from £195 to £298; ask your council which applies. Most councils pay more and add a carer's fee. Our pay pages show, for example, that Suffolk's child allowance for 2026-27 runs from £187 to £274 a week before any fee, and we also publish what Luton, Central Bedfordshire and Thurrock pay.

For local detail, start with the page for your county or town, such as Essex, Norfolk, Suffolk, Hertfordshire, Cambridgeshire, Bedfordshire or Peterborough, then speak to your council and to two or three of the services above.
