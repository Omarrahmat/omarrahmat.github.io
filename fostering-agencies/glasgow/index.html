Fostering in Glasgow

In Glasgow, the council's fostering and adoption service, Families for Children, part of Glasgow City Health and Social Care Partnership, approves carers to look after children who cannot live with their own families, alongside independent fostering agencies such as those listed on this page. Glasgow City Council says the number of looked-after children living in foster homes has risen and that it needs more people to come forward, from all walks of life and whether or not they have children of their own.

You can apply on your own or as a couple; couples do not need to be married or in a civil partnership but must have lived together for at least two years. Every applicant goes through council, health and employment checks and the Protection of Vulnerable Groups (PVG) scheme. Foster carers work as part of a team with social workers, other services and, where appropriate, children's parents.

Not all fostering in Glasgow is full time. The partnership also recruits short breaks carers, who give a child or family a regular break, such as one night a week or a weekend a month, and who often combine this with a full-time job. Short breaks carers need to be over 21 and have a spare bedroom; no qualifications are required and training and support are provided.

Before deciding, request the council's information pack, speak to Families for Children and to two or three of the agencies below, and compare the training, support and allowances each offers.

Sources: Foster a child (Glasgow City Council); Have you ever thought about fostering, but ruled it out because you work? (Glasgow City HSCP, January 2024).
