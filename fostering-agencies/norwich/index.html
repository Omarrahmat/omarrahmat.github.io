Fostering in Norwich

Children in care in Norwich are the responsibility of Norfolk County Council, whose not-for-profit Norfolk Fostering Service has a local office in the city as well as in King's Lynn, Thetford and Great Yarmouth. The independent agencies shown above also place children here. The council says around five children come into care in Norfolk every week, and in January 2026 its cabinet member for children said the county urgently needed another 50 foster carers.

The council fosters children from birth to 18 and offers a wide range of roles: sleepover and short-term care, long-term homes, parent and child placements, short breaks for disabled children, emergency care, supported lodgings for 16 to 18 year olds and homes for unaccompanied asylum-seeking children. One option is particular to the city: New Roads, paid shift work in residential hubs in Norwich and Dereham supporting young people in care or on the edge of care.

New council carers are given a therapist and a supervising social worker for their first two years, training before approval through the Skills to Foster course and a free programme of local training afterwards, support around the clock, mentoring, support groups and membership of The Fostering Network. Norfolk is also part of Foster East, a group of 11 councils in the East of England that share recruitment and specialist courses.

Norfolk does not publish a full dated allowance scale that we could check, so ask the council and two or three of the agencies above what they pay, how they support carers locally and what training they offer before you decide.

Sources: Why foster for the council (Norfolk County Council); Types of fostering (Norfolk County Council); Training and support (Norfolk County Council); Adam Buxton shines a light on the need for more foster carers in new documentary (Norfolk County Council, 6 January 2026).
