Fostering in Sheffield

Sheffield City Council runs its own fostering service and says it urgently needs more foster carers. Ofsted inspected the service in September 2023 and rated it good overall, with the experiences and progress of children in care judged outstanding. Children from Sheffield may also live with independent agencies such as those listed on this page.

The council offers short-term, long-term, emergency, respite and parent and baby fostering, and also recruits Supported Lodgings hosts for young people aged 16 to 21.

To apply you need to be at least 21, live in the UK full time and have the time to care for a child; there is no upper age limit and no qualifications are needed. You can be single, rent your home, have a job, receive benefits, have a disability, have pets or be LGBTQ+. Smokers can foster, though not children under five or children with health conditions or disabilities. You do not need to drive, but you must be able to get children to school, appointments and family time. Some past convictions unrelated to children may not rule you out.

The council promises training, advice and support around the clock. You can ask for an information pack with no obligation or join one of its online information evenings.

Before deciding, compare what the council offers with two or three of the agencies below, and ask each about pay, training and the support you would get in your part of the city.

Sources: Welcome to Sheffield Fostering Service (Sheffield City Council); Who can foster (Sheffield City Council); Celebrating the power of relationships in fostering, May 2025 (Sheffield City Council).
