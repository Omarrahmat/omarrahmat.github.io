Fostering in Northumberland

Children in care from Northumberland are looked after by the county council's own service, Foster with Northumberland, and by the independent agencies listed on this page. The council tries to keep children close to their communities, schools, friends and family where that is safe, so it looks for carers who live in the county or nearby. Placements range from short and medium term to permanent homes until adulthood.

Applicants normally need to be 25 or over, although people aged 21 to 24 with substantial experience of caring work can be considered, and there is no upper age limit. You need a spare bedroom, but it does not matter whether you own or rent, whether you are single or in a couple, or whether you work. Adults aged 16 and over in the household have enhanced DBS checks, and internet access and a way to get children to school and family time are essential.

The process starts with an informal chat with Foster with North East, the regional hub, followed by a home visit, usually within a week of registering. A two-day Skills to Foster course runs early in the assessment, and the council says most applicants reach its fostering panel within three to six months.

For April 2026 to March 2027, Northumberland's weekly allowance for each child runs from £176 for a child aged 0 to 2 to £267 for a young person aged 16 or 17. On top of that, carers receive a weekly fee that rises from £117 in Band 1 to £289 in Band 3 as they gain skills and experience. Our Northumberland allowance page has the full table. Carers also have a dedicated fostering social worker, out-of-hours support, and the chance to join the council's Mockingbird constellations, run with The Fostering Network, where linked families support each other like an extended family.

If you live in or near Northumberland, speak to the council and two or three of the agencies below, and compare their training, support and pay before you decide.
