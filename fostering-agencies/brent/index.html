Fostering in Brent

Brent Council fosters for its own children, and the independent agencies shown above also approve carers who live in the borough. Choosing the council means caring for Brent children close to home with Brent's social workers supporting you; choosing an agency means it assesses, trains and supervises you, and councils, Brent among them, place children with its carers.

Brent's annual corporate parenting report for 2025-26 gives the need. At 31 March 2026 the council looked after 295 children, 180 of them in foster care, and its own mainstream carers were looking after 49. One in five children lived more than 20 miles from home. The children are mostly older: 70.5% were aged 10 or over and 32.5% were 16 or over, and 30 were unaccompanied asylum-seeking children. The report says local homes are hardest to find for adolescents with highly complex needs. Brent had 95 fostering households offering 132 places, up from 88 and 128 a year before, but approved three new mainstream households against its target of ten.

Interest is rising. The council's six-monthly fostering report says a digital campaign lifted enquiries to Foster with West London, the recruitment partnership Brent shares with seven neighbouring councils, from around 30 to around 120 a month. It describes the first stage of assessment as taking 8 to 10 weeks and the second four to six months. A cabinet report in July 2026 moved the partnership to carry out assessments and training for all eight councils from the autumn, and says people are encouraged to foster for the council where they live and pay council tax.

Brent says its carers get one-to-one support from an experienced social worker, telephone support 24 hours a day, a monthly peer support group, free therapeutic support, a free laptop or tablet, help with a bed, cot and car seat, Fostering Network membership and annual social events. Mockingbird, in which a group of fostering families is supported by an experienced hub carer, went live in May 2025.

To foster for the council you must be at least 21, live in Brent or a neighbouring borough and have a spare room for an older child or space for a cot for a baby under two. Renters, single people and couples can all apply, and you can keep working if your job leaves you free for school runs and appointments. We do not yet publish a Brent allowance page, so ask the council and the agencies above for their weekly rates. The Harrow, Ealing, Barnet and London pages list more services nearby.

Sources: Annual Corporate Parenting Report 2025-26, Corporate Parenting Committee 14 July 2026 (Brent Council); Brent Fostering Service 6-monthly Monitoring Report, October 2025 to March 2026 (Brent Council); West London Fostering Recruitment and Retention Programme, Cabinet 27 July 2026 (Brent Council); Foster with West London (Brent Council); Fostering with Brent (Brent Council); Who can become a foster carer (Brent Council).
