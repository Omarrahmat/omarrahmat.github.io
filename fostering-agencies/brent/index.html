Fostering in Brent

Brent Council's own fostering service and the independent agencies that list Brent as an area they cover are shown above. Fostering for the council means caring for Brent's own children, supported by its social workers; an independent agency assesses, trains and supervises you itself, and takes children placed with it by councils, Brent included.

Brent's enquiries go through Foster with West London, a partnership of eight councils: Brent, Ealing, Hammersmith and Fulham, Harrow, Hillingdon, Hounslow, Kensington and Chelsea, and Westminster. It launched in May 2024 to handle enquiries and marketing, and a cabinet report in July 2026 moved it to an end-to-end service, taking on assessment and training of new carers from 1 October 2026, with Hammersmith and Fulham as lead council. The report says the partnership has approved 41 households across the region since its launch. Carers are encouraged to foster for the council where they live.

The council's annual corporate parenting report for 2025-26 shows why it wants more local carers. On 31 March 2026 Brent looked after 295 children, 180 of them in foster care, and 49 of those children lived with the council's own mainstream carers. One in five children (20%) were placed more than 20 miles from home. Most children in Brent's care are older: 70.5% were 10 or over and almost a third were 16 or over, and 30 were unaccompanied asylum-seeking children. The council approved three new mainstream fostering households in the year against a target of ten, and its sufficiency strategy for 2025 to 2029 names older teenagers, children with complex needs and brothers and sisters as the hardest to place. Carers can join the Mockingbird peer-support scheme, which Brent launched in May 2025.

To foster for the council you need to be at least 21 and live in Brent or a neighbouring borough, with a spare room for an older child or space for a cot for a baby up to two. You can own your home or rent it, be single, married or in a relationship, and keep working if your job leaves you free for school runs and doctor's appointments. Enquiries go to Foster with West London, which now carries out the assessment on Brent's behalf.

Compare the council with the agencies above, and ask each what it pays and how it supports carers of teenagers. If you live near the borough's edge, the Harrow, Ealing, Barnet and London pages list more services.

Sources: Foster with West London (Brent Council); Which local authority partners are working together in Foster with West London (Foster with West London); West London Fostering Recruitment and Retention Programme, Cabinet 27 July 2026 (Brent Council); Annual Corporate Parenting Report 2025-26, Corporate Parenting Committee 14 July 2026 (Brent Council); Looked After Children and Care Leavers Sufficiency Strategy 2025-2029, cover report, 2 February 2026 (Brent Council); Who can become a foster carer (Brent Council).
