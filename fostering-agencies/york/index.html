Fostering in York

City of York Council runs its own fostering service, Fostering York, and the independent agencies shown above also approve carers in and around the city. With the council you care for York's own children, often close to their schools and friends; an agency assesses, approves and supports its own carers and takes children whom councils, York among them, ask it to place.

The council's sufficiency strategy for 2024 to 2027 says York had 243 children in care at the end of March 2024, and that just under half of them, 46.91 per cent, were living in York. It names teenagers as the most challenging children to find homes for, notes a rising share of unaccompanied children seeking asylum, and sets the aim of 50 more fostering households over five years, with a predicted need of 10 to 15 new carers a year, so that the council relies less on agencies. In March 2025 the council's Executive considered a new fee and skills framework with four levels for carers; the report then counted 65 council fostering households caring for 84 children, 40 children placed with agencies and 74 living with kinship carers.

Fostering York's information guide for 2026 to 2027 covers short breaks, long-term and short-term care, emergency placements, parent and child placements and children who may go on to be adopted, and says the council is always looking for carers who can keep brothers and sisters together. Becoming a carer starts with an information session or call, a home visit and the Skills to Foster course, then a first assessment stage of around two months with checks and references and a second of around four months, before the fostering panel. Approved carers have a supervising social worker, out-of-hours help, the York Area Foster Carer Association, Fostering Network membership and a yearly development plan.

In October 2025 the council thanked seven households with 20 to 40 years of fostering between them, who had cared for more than 600 local children, and in March 2026 it set aside part of York Community Woodland for foster carers. Speak to the council and two or three of the services above about pay and support; we do not yet publish a York pay page. The North Yorkshire, East Riding of Yorkshire and Leeds pages list more services nearby.

Sources: Sufficiency Strategy 2024-2027, Annex A (City of York Council); Executive report: Fostering Framework (City of York Council, Executive 11 March 2025); Fostering information guide 2026/2027 (Fostering York); The steps to becoming an approved foster carer (Fostering York); Inspiring commitment of York's longest-serving foster carers celebrated (City of York Council, 15 October 2025); Photo story: York woodland supports work of York's foster carers (City of York Council, 24 March 2026).
