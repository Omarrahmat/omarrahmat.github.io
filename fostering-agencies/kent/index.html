Fostering in Kent

Kent County Council's own fostering service, Kent Fostering, looks after children across the county, and the independent agencies shown above also recruit carers here. The council's 2025 corporate parenting report speaks of 1,966 children and young people in its care. On 22 September 2025, 650 children were living in 510 mainstream fostering households, and a further 106 were with relatives or family friends in 81 kinship fostering households. Kinship carers now make up 13.6% of Kent's foster carers.

Interest is rising but the need has not gone away. The same report records a 4% rise in enquiries, from 736 to 764, while applications fell by 135 on the year before. It also notes that 7.8% of the children in Kent's care are Black, yet carers from Black and mixed backgrounds are only 6% of its foster carers, and it marks the target of placing children within 20 miles of home as behind, partly because unaccompanied children under 16 keep arriving and need foster homes. Kent Fostering asks for families from every community, whatever their ethnicity, sexual orientation, disability, age or marital status, and says no specific qualifications are needed.

Fostering for the council means caring for Kent's own children, with its social workers supervising you; an agency assesses, trains and supports its carers itself and looks after children that councils ask it to place. Kent's sufficiency strategy, refreshed in 2024, points to a national shortage of foster carers and aims to keep children within the county unless that is not reasonably practicable. Since July 2024 Kent has also been one of 20 councils in Local Authority Fostering South East, a Department for Education funded hub where people can make a first enquiry in one place and speak to an existing carer through its ambassador scheme; 550 of the hub's 2,915 enquiries were for Kent.

Our Kent allowance page has the council's 2026-27 rates. The weekly allowance for each child runs from £210 for a child under two to £320.92 for a young person over 16. Carers looking after a child who is not related to them also receive a weekly reward payment of £156.70 for a child aged up to 8 or £297.64 for a child aged 9 to 18, with skills payments of £28.09 at Level 2 and £70.15 at Level 3, and a disability enhancement where it applies. Agencies set their own fees, so ask each service what it pays and what support comes with it.

If you live near the county's edges, the Medway, East Sussex, Surrey and Bexley pages list more services, and the South East of England page covers the wider region.
