Fostering in Southampton

Children in care in Southampton are looked after by Southampton City Council's fostering service and by independent agencies such as the services shown above. In a Foster Care Fortnight appeal the council said the number of children coming into its care keeps rising and that it needs more fostering households from the city and the area around it, so that children can stay close to their family, friends and school. Carers who can look after older children and teenagers are the ones it said it most needs.

Carers approved by the council have their own supervising social worker, round-the-clock help from the Emergency Duty Team, support groups and social events, and membership of the Southampton Foster Carers Association and FosterTalk; LGBTQ+ carers can also join New Family Social. Training covers subjects such as attachment and trauma and supporting a child with special educational needs, carers can move up through the council's fostering skills levels, and there are extra payments for specialist fostering. We have not yet published a Southampton pay page, so ask the council for its current fees and allowances when you compare it with an agency.

The council is recognised by The Fostering Network as a fostering friendly employer: its own staff who foster can work flexibly and have up to five extra days of paid leave a year for training, settling a child in and other fostering commitments, and it encourages Southampton businesses to do the same.

Anyone over 21 can apply, whether or not they have children of their own; marital status, sexuality, education and disability do not decide it, and the council will ask about any experience of looking after children and about a spare bedroom. After the online form, the first checks (references, a DBS check, council checks and a medical with your GP) generally take around two months. A social worker then completes the Form F assessment in your home, usually within four months, alongside Skills to Foster training, before you go to the approval panel.

Fostering with the council means caring for Southampton's own children with its team behind you; an agency approves, trains and supports you itself, and councils, Southampton among them, ask it to care for children who need a home. Speak to the council and two or three of the services above about the children who most need homes and the support each offers. If you live outside the city, see our pages for Hampshire, Portsmouth and the Isle of Wight.
