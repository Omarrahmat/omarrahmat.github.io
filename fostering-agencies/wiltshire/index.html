Fostering in Wiltshire

Wiltshire Council looks after around 500 children and young people, according to its May 2026 fostering update. It has its own fostering service, recruiting through the Fostering South West partnership of council services, and children from the county may also live with independent agencies such as those on this page.

The council says carers are most needed for older children and teenagers, for brothers and sisters who need to stay together, for children who have arrived in the UK separated from their families, and for emergency placements at short notice.

You can be single, married, working or retired. You need to be 21 or over with a spare bedroom, although a baby up to 12 months can sleep in your room if there is space for a cot.

In its 2025 recruitment news the council says carers receive weekly payments of £410 to £760 per child, depending on skills and experience. Emergency carers, who are available for at least 14 nights a month, receive £900 a week when a child is placed and £500 a week when they are available but not caring for a child. The council also offers training and a dedicated support team.

The council runs information sessions and drop-ins across the county. Compare its offer with two or three of the agencies below before you decide.

Sources: This Is Fostering in Wiltshire: how local carers are changing lives (Wiltshire Council, May 2026); Wiltshire Council calls for more foster carers to support teenagers in need; Wiltshire Council urges people to foster and help keep brothers and sisters together; Can you provide an emergency home for a young person? (Wiltshire Council).
