Fostering in Stockton

Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council runs its own fostering service, shown above with the independent agencies that list Stockton as an area they cover. The council says it needs carers from all walks of life for babies, children, teenagers and sibling groups.

Stockton is part of Foster with North East, a regional partnership of North East councils backed by the Department for Education. First enquiries can go through the regional hub, which sends out an information pack and runs information sessions online and in person, while your application and assessment stay with Stockton's own fostering team. Hub carers also offer extra support from the first application onwards.

The council asks that carers are aged 25 or over, although people aged 21 to 25 may be considered in some circumstances. You need a spare bedroom suitable for a child, although a baby can sleep in a cot in your room, and you must live in the UK full time or have leave to remain. Fostering often means being available full time, so time and commitment matter as much as space.

Stockton has also introduced the Mockingbird family model, in which a group of up to ten foster families is linked to a specially trained hub home carer. Families in a group get planned breaks and emergency sleepovers for the children, regular social events, monthly coffee mornings and mentoring from other carers. The council says the model helps keep siblings in touch and makes placements more stable, and it wants to set up more of these groups.

Talk to the council and to two or three of the services above, and ask each about pay, training and support before you decide.

Sources: Fostering (Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council); Become a foster carer (Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council); Mockingbird (Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council).
