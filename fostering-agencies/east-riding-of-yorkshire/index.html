Fostering in East Riding of Yorkshire

East Riding of Yorkshire Council fosters children from Beverley, Bridlington, Goole, Withernsea, Market Weighton and the villages across the county, and independent agencies such as the services shown above also look after children from the area. The council says babies, children and young people of every age need foster carers, and that local carers let children stay close to the schools, friends and places they already know. In May 2025 it said it had more than 100 foster families and needed more.

The council asks that carers are UK residents with a spare bedroom, and ideally aged 25 or over. You do not need qualifications, to own your home or to have children of your own, and you do not have to live in the East Riding. Fostering can mean one night, several years or a home until adulthood.

The support it describes is distinctive: social workers, training and local support groups, regular social events for carers' families, and free East Riding Leisure membership for the whole household. The council calls its financial package one of the most competitive in Yorkshire and the Humber, with increased allowances, equipment contributions and birthday and Christmas payments. Its published policy adds a holiday allowance of two weeks' basic allowance a year, a weekly supplement for children aged 13 and over, skills fees by banding and an annual payment on the anniversary of approval, with rates reviewed each April. It does not publish the cash figures, so ask for them.

The council holds information sessions across the county, often during Foster Care Fortnight in May. Compare what it offers with two or three of the agencies above before you apply.

Sources: East Riding Fostering (East Riding of Yorkshire Council); Fostering allowances (East Riding Children's Services procedures); Foster Care Fortnight celebrates the power of relationships in East Riding (HU17, May 2025); Fostering information sessions across the East Riding (Just Beverley, May 2023); Urgent need for more foster carers in the East Riding (Hello Rayo, May 2022).
