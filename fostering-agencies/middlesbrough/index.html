Fostering in Middlesbrough

Children in care in Middlesbrough are looked after by foster families approved either by Middlesbrough Council's own fostering service or by independent fostering services, such as the agencies shown above. The council now recruits carers together with the other councils in the North East through Foster with North East, a regional hub backed by the Department for Education, so enquiries to the council are made through that hub.

The council says there are children in Middlesbrough who need someone to give them a safe, stable home, and it describes foster carers as much needed. It welcomes people from all walks of life, reflecting the wide range of needs the children it looks after have.

On support, the council describes an in-house fostering team that stays with applicants from the start, a named social worker for each fostering household and an out-of-hours emergency team. New carers can be paired with an experienced carer through a buddy scheme, and there are support groups, a carers' association and the Fostering Network's Mockingbird programme, which links fostering households in a way similar to an extended family. Training mixes required and optional courses, with a dedicated training officer. The council says its allowances are competitive and regularly reviewed, but it does not publish the amounts on these pages, so it is worth asking directly.

To get started, you could compare Middlesbrough Council with two or three of the services on this page, asking each about training, the support you would receive and how allowances are worked out. The regional hub also runs information events where you can learn about the application process before deciding.

Sources: Middlesbrough Council: Fostering; Middlesbrough Council: Foster with North East; Foster with North East: Middlesbrough Council; Foster with North East: Information event with Middlesbrough Council.
