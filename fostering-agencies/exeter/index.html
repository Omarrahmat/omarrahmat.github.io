Fostering in Exeter

Children in care in Exeter are looked after by Devon County Council's own fostering service, Fostering Devon, and by the independent agencies shown above. Devon is short of local foster homes: in May 2026 the council said it had around 810 children in care and around 271 approved foster homes, and that it was losing more carers than it was gaining. It also said more than 30 people were then going through approval.

The council has changed the way it recruits, with more open conversations, real carers' experiences and work with local communities and employers. One example was in Exeter: in March 2026 Fostering Devon held its Hearts and Homes conference at the Future Skills Centre in Exeter, with talks from experienced foster carers, workshops on the Skills to Foster course and therapeutic parenting, and information on how employers can support staff who foster. The council also offers a no-obligation call to anyone who cannot get to an event.

Devon says you can apply if you are over 21 and have a spare bedroom, and that carers can be single or in a couple, working or retired. Its carers get training and support, financial help and possible savings on council tax. In September 2025 the council set out further improvements for carers, including a new online carer portal, changes to the way payments and expenses are handled, a yearly celebration designed with carers and continued backing for the Mockingbird programme, which links groups of foster families with an experienced hub carer.

Before you decide, speak to Fostering Devon and to two or three of the agencies above, and ask each about training, support and payments for the children you could look after.

Sources: New approach to recruiting foster carers in Devon showing early impact (Devon County Council, May 2026); Celebrating Devon's foster carers in Fostering Fortnight 2026 (Devon County Council); Local people invited to explore fostering at Exeter event (Devon County Council, March 2026); Devon County Council strengthens support for foster carers with new measures and ongoing improvements (September 2025).
