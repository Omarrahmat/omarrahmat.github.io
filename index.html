Landlords, spare rooms and tenancies explainedRenting does not stop you fostering. What matters is a settled home, a bedroom a child can call their own and, in most cases, your landlord's written agreement. Being single does not stop you fostering anywhere in the UK. What fostering services look at is your home, your time and the people around you. Here is what single applicants are asked, how single carers make it work, and what to sort out before you apply.→09Can you choose the age of child you foster?Yes, in most cases. You agree the ages and number of children you will look after with your fostering service during your assessment, and that becomes part of your approval. You can also turn down a child who would not fit your family. But the more open you are, especially to older children and brothers and sisters, the sooner you are likely to be matched. Here is how it works across the UK.→10Can you foster if you don't drive?Usually, yes. Most UK fostering services say a driving licence is helpful but not essential, as long as you can show how you would get a child to school, family time, appointments and meetings. A few services do ask for a driver with a car. Here is what services say, how non-drivers make it work and what drivers need to know about insurance and mileage.→11Can you foster if you smoke or vape?Yes, in many cases, but smoking narrows the children you can care for. Most fostering services will not place children under five, disabled children or children with breathing or heart conditions with a smoking household, and many treat vaping the same way. Here is what the guidance says, how services apply it and what changes if you have recently quit.→12Can you foster with a criminal record?Often, yes. A conviction or caution does not automatically rule you out of fostering, but a small group of serious offences, mostly against children, do. Here is what the regulations say, what the checks involve in each UK nation, and how services weigh up everything else.→13Do you need a spare bedroom to foster?For most types of fostering, yes: a child placed with you will usually need a bedroom of their own. But the rules have more room in them than many people think, for babies, brothers and sisters, short breaks and small rooms. Here is what official guidance across the UK actually says.→14What is emergency fostering, and could you do it?Emergency foster carers take in a child at very short notice, often in the evening, overnight or at a weekend, and look after them for a few nights or a couple of weeks while longer-term plans are made. Here is how it works, what services ask of emergency carers, how they are paid and supported, and how the rules differ across the UK.→15Can you work and foster? Fostering alongside a jobYes, many foster carers also have a job. Whether it works for you depends on the type of fostering, the age of the child, your hours and how flexible your employer is. Here is what the role involves, who it suits, how training, pay and benefits work, and how short breaks fostering differs across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.→17Can you foster with a health condition?Usually, yes. No health condition, physical or mental, automatically rules you out of fostering. Every applicant has a medical, and the fostering service looks at whether you can safely care for a child, now and over the years ahead. Here is how the medical works and what services take into account in each UK nation.→18How long does it take to become a foster carer?Most people are approved between six months and a year after applying. Here is where the time goes, the legal time limits in England, how things differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, and what you can do to avoid delays.→19Kinship fostering and special guardianship: caring for a child you already knowIf a grandchild, niece, nephew or a family friend's child cannot live with their parents, you may be asked to step in. 12 November 2026PolicyEngland

Government plan aims to halve homelessness among young people leaving care in England

A Care Leaver Homelessness Reduction Action Plan published on 5 November sets two national targets, new duties on councils and fresh guidance, including on young people who stay with their foster carers after 18.

11 November 2026WalesRegulation

Wales sets out rules to carry foster carer approvals over when agencies go not-for-profit

Welsh Government impact assessments published on 10 November describe new regulations letting carers keep their approval when a for-profit fostering service re-forms as a not-for-profit body, instead of being assessed again.

9 November 2026EnglandCouncils

Private Fostering Awareness Day 2026: councils remind families caring for someone else's child to tell them

Councils marked Private Fostering Awareness Day on 4 November by reminding parents and carers that arrangements lasting 28 days or more must be reported, as CoramBAAF published new findings from 48 local authorities.
